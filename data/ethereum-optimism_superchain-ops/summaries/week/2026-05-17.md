# ethereum-optimism/superchain-ops Weekly Report (May 17 - 23, 2026)

## 🚀 Highlights
This week, development focused heavily on the technical maturation of the Interop hard fork and the refinement of Superchain testing infrastructure. Significant progress was made in aligning `kona-node` and `op-node` implementations, specifically resolving critical discrepancies in Brotli channel handling and transitioning the supervisor component to a new interop filter namespace. Efforts also centered on stabilizing the CI pipeline and resolving flaky end-to-end tests to ensure a reliable foundation for the upcoming Interop Proofs devnet.

## 🛠️ Key Developments

### Interop Hard Fork & Protocol Alignment
The team completed several core tasks required for the Interop hard fork, focusing on protocol consistency across different node implementations.
*   **L2CM Integration:** Successfully implemented the Interop hard fork using the L2 Chain Manager (L2CM) within both `kona-node` and `op-node` [#20082].
*   **Namespace Migration:** The supervisor RPC namespace was officially replaced with an interop filter namespace, streamlining how interop-related data is queried [#20005].
*   **Brotli Channel Consistency:** Resolved a significant discrepancy where `Kona` and `op-node` disagreed on Brotli channel decompression. This was addressed via a detailed investigation and the implementation of a wrapper fix to ensure cross-client compatibility [#19333].

### Infrastructure & CI Refinement
Work continued on optimizing the developer experience and the reliability of automated testing environments.
*   **CI Streamlining:** A new PR [#1429](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/superchain-ops/pull/1429) was opened to refine CircleCI configurations by removing the `circleci_ip_ranges` flag and routing fork-sim RPCs through the secrets context.
*   **Test Suite Migration:** Dispute game tests were migrated from `op-e2e/super_test.go` to the more specialized `op-acceptance-tests` repository to improve test organization and execution [#20814].
*   **Code Auditing:** An investigation into `debug_executePayload` concluded that the associated crates are still required as an active opt-in fast-path for `op-reth`, preventing the accidental removal of necessary code [#20879].

## 🐛 Issues & Triage

### Closed Issues
*   **Protocol Stability:** Beyond the Interop and Brotli fixes, the team resolved issues surrounding the removal of the supervisor component, marking a shift toward the new interop architecture.
*   **Test Reliability:** Root causes for several flaky tests were identified and mitigated, including timing issues in the `PreGenesisGame` where migration budgets were being exceeded [#20869].

### New & Active Issues
*   **Devnet Readiness:** Activity is high regarding the deployment of the Interop Proofs devnet [#20489], which will test SuperRootMigrations and flashblock compatibility. Current efforts are focused on spinning up local environments using `bal-devnet-6` images while awaiting `glamsterdam-devnet-4` images [#20691].
*   **Integration Blockers:** The integration of SDM with flashblocks flows [#20175] remains blocked pending the resolution of upstream issues in the core optimism repository.
*   **Ongoing Flakes:** A fix is currently in progress for `TestSupernodeInterop_SafeHeadProgression` [#20851] following a pipeline failure.

## 💬 Community & Collaboration
The week's activity demonstrates tight coordination between protocol engineers and tooling developers, particularly in the synchronization of `Kona` (the Rust-based rollup state transition function) and the standard `op-node`. The resolution of the Brotli discrepancy and the investigation into `op-reth` dependencies highlight a collaborative effort to maintain cross-client compatibility. Furthermore, the proactive fixing of CI flakes (e.g., [#20874]) indicates a strong commitment to maintaining a healthy and unblocked contributor pipeline.
