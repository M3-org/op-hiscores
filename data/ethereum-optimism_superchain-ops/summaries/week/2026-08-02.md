# ethereum-optimism/superchain-ops Weekly Report (Aug 2 - 8, 2026)

## 🚀 Highlights
This week focused on critical ownership transitions and infrastructure maintenance within the Superchain ecosystem, notably the L1 ownership transfer for Swell Mainnet. Operations also centered on refining the Ink network's configuration, including proposer rotations and fee vault updates. While progress was made on CI optimization and resolving `op-reth` proof history lags, the team continues to navigate complex technical hurdles regarding `op-node` synchronization and SDM performance benchmarking.

## 🛠️ Key Developments

### Superchain Governance & Ownership Transfers
Significant effort was directed toward executing approved maintenance proposals for Superchain partners.
*   **Swell Mainnet:** Successfully executed the L1 component of the ownership transfer to AltLayer, involving comprehensive updates to configuration and address files ([#1511](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/superchain-ops/pull/1511)).
*   **Ink Network:** Initiated the transfer of the Ink `SystemConfig` ownership from FOS to FUS ([#1515](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/superchain-ops/pull/1515)).

### Network Operations & Task Management
Routine operational tasks were documented and updated to ensure transparency in network rotations and game type registrations.
*   **Ink Maintenance:** Updated the status for Task 061 (Proposer Rotation) and Task 062 (Fee Vault Recipient Update) within their respective READMEs ([#1513](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/superchain-ops/pull/1513)).
*   **Game Type Registration:** Opened a new task for the registration of RECA Standard game types ([#1514](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/superchain-ops/pull/1514)).

### Infrastructure & CI Improvements
*   **CI Optimization:** Resolved issues regarding CI makespan. The team decided to prioritize merging smaller, individual PRs to improve pipeline efficiency rather than attempting to optimize single large PRs ([#21186](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/superchain-ops/issues/21186)).

## 🐛 Issues & Triage

### Closed Issues
*   **op-reth Proofs History:** Addressed a permanent lag in `op-reth` proofs history following clean shutdowns. The resolution determined that consistency should be managed at startup rather than through shutdown logic changes, as the proofs exex naturally catches up as the chain produces blocks ([#22089](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/superchain-ops/issues/22089)).

### New & Active Issues
*   **SDM Performance & Rollout:** The `sdm-net-0` rollout is nearing completion. Current focus has shifted to benchmarking the overhead of SDM-aware chains using the `op-benchmark` tool on SSD devnets. Key remaining gaps include contract/transaction load generation and porting the SDM Grafana dashboard ([#20176](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/superchain-ops/issues/20176), [#21273](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/superchain-ops/issues/21273)).
*   **Node Synchronization:** Persistent peering challenges remain for `op-node` when `syncmode=execution-layer` is used with an `op-reth` backend. Users reported frequent `Ecies` stream errors, though some pods eventually established minimal peering after several days ([#20974](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/superchain-ops/issues/20974)).
*   **Integration Test Flakes:** Investigation into a `SIGSEGV` crash during the `peers_negotiate_eth_69` test teardown is currently paused. The team has instrumented CI to capture a core dump during the next occurrence to identify the root cause ([#20973](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/superchain-ops/issues/20973)).

## 💬 Community & Collaboration
The week's activity reflects a high level of coordination between the Optimism core team and ecosystem partners like AltLayer and Swell. Technical discussions around SDM (Superchain Dependency Manager) indicate a collaborative effort to refine benchmarking tools, with contributors suggesting the use of existing rudimentary scripts to unblock traffic generation. The decision-making process regarding CI makespan highlights a community preference for modular, incremental development over monolithic updates.
