## ACTIVE ISSUES

### op-node: On the Initial EL Sync UX and Correctness ([#17631](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/superchain-ops/issues/17631))
Discussion continues on the behavior of `op-node` marking a block as finalized after initial EL Sync, even if it's not truly finalized. `trianglesphere` proposed several options for a finalization gadget, including a command-line flag, an RPC URL for a trusted checkpoint provider, looking at L1 for the last finalized head, or an arbitrary delay of the finalized head (12 hours to 10 days) after EL sync. `karlfloersch` favored the operational simplicity of delaying safe/finalized by the sequencing window. `trianglesphere` also clarified that this behavior was the intended design for EL sync, and that re-running derivation on a snap-synced node doesn't work as it can only execute blocks after snap syncing.

### non-terminating CI job: memory-all-opn-op-geth ([#19563](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/superchain-ops/issues/19563))
`ajsutton` reported another occurrence of a timeout, specifically `TestChallengerPlaysGame` after 2 hours. The root cause was identified as RPC calls within polling loops using the base test context with a 2-hour deadline, leading to indefinite blocking when L1 geth hangs due to CI resource contention. A draft fix ([#19634](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/optimism/pull/19634)) was opened to create context-bound copies of the `FaultDisputeGame` binding for polling loops, ensuring RPC calls respect per-wait timeouts. `ajsutton` also noted that the `interop/proofs` package is timing out due to acceptance tests creating full L1+L2+supernode systems per test without shared setup, causing cumulative runtime to exceed the 2-hour job timeout.

### flaky test: `TestBroadcastWithSlowClient` ([#19584](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/superchain-ops/issues/19584))
`ajsutton` completed the investigation, identifying the root cause as the test blasting small messages through real WebSocket connections, which under CI load, exceeded the `writePump` capacity of both clients, leading to drops. The fix ([#19594](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/optimism/pull/19594)) involves using larger messages to fill OS socket buffers quickly, pacing sends, and using an `OnMessageBroadcast` callback to detect drops.

### flaky test: `TestSuperchain` ([#19586](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/superchain-ops/issues/19586))
`ajsutton` concluded the investigation, finding the root cause to be `t.Parallel()` in `testSuperchain`, causing concurrent execution of `mainnet` and `sepolia` subtests. This led to resource contention and timeouts. The fix ([#19582](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/optimism/pull/19582)) involved removing `t.Parallel()`.

### flaky test: `TestAddressValidDeployment` ([#19591](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/superchain-ops/issues/19591))
`ajsutton` completed the investigation, attributing the flakiness to live RPC calls to `publicnode.com` endpoints for mainnet and sepolia, which intermittently timed out or experienced connection resets under CI load. The fix ([#19592](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/optimism/pull/19592)) adds retry logic with a delay for transient errors, consolidates RPC clients, and increases the context timeout.
