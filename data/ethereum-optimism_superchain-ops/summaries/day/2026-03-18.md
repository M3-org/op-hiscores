### Initial EL Sync UX and Correctness
Discussion on [#17631](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/superchain-ops/issues/17631) continued, focusing on the behavior of `op-node` marking a block as finalized after initial EL Sync, even if it's not truly finalized.
- **trianglesphere** proposed using the L1 finalized head timestamp minus a 12-hour sequence window to ensure the finalized head is not set ahead of derivation, though this doesn't guarantee consistency.
- **karlfloersch** favored the operational simplicity of delaying the finalized head by the sequencing window (12 hours to 10 days) as a solution.
- **trianglesphere** further elaborated on a proposal to continue EL sync to the unsafe head and use a separate finalization gadget. Several options for this gadget were suggested, including a command-line flag, a trusted checkpoint provider RPC, looking at L1 for the last finalized head, or an arbitrary delay. The command-line flag or 12-hour fallback were preferred. It was also noted that this would remove derivation ability on `op-geth` but is acceptable due to its deprecation.

### Non-terminating CI job: memory-all-opn-op-geth
The issue [#19563](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/superchain-ops/issues/19563) regarding the `memory-all-opn-op-geth` CI job timing out continued to be investigated.
- **ajsutton** reported another occurrence where `TestChallengerPlaysGame` timed out after 2 hours, with CircleCI Insights showing 3 flakes for this test. An investigation was launched.
- **ajsutton** opened a draft fix in a separate PR, identifying the root cause as polling loops in `waitForClaim` and `WaitForCountered` using a 20-minute timeout context, while RPC calls within them used the base test context with a 2-hour deadline. This led to indefinite blocking when RPC calls to the in-memory L1 geth hung due to CI resource contention. The fix involves creating a context-bound copy of the `FaultDisputeGame` binding for polling loops.
- **ajsutton** also noted that the `interop/proofs` package was timing out in the same job, with several `TestProposer` and `TestChallenger` tests showing flakes. Logs indicated `l2-proposer` stuck retrying and `supernode` error loops, attributing these to acceptance tests creating full L1+L2+supernode systems per test without shared setup, leading to excessive runtime.
- **geoknee** mentioned that several ongoing PRs, including #19571, might resolve this issue and suggested re-evaluating the problem's persistence in a few days.

### Flaky test: `TestSupernodeSameTimestampInvalidTransitive`
The flaky test `TestSupernodeSameTimestampInvalidTransitive` in [#19570](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/superchain-ops/issues/19570) was reopened and investigated.
- **maurelian** provided a detailed root cause analysis, identifying a consistent timeout at ~2m46s. The failure mode is a race condition in multi-chain transitive invalidation. When chain B's rewind completes, it can "poison" geth's block rejection cache, causing chain A's subsequent synthetic payload insertion to be rejected with "links to previously rejected block." The `RewindEngine` then retries indefinitely because `ErrRewindSyntheticPayloadRejected` is not considered a critical error.
- **maurelian** outlined potential fix directions, including treating `ErrRewindSyntheticPayloadRejected` as critical, clearing geth's block rejection cache before rewind, using a different rewind strategy if the synthetic payload fails, or coordinating multi-chain rewinds to prevent cross-chain cache poisoning.
- **maurelian** also added CI run context, noting the error "Test timed out after 2m46s (`assertions.go:387: Received unexpected error`)" from a `memory-all-opn-op-geth` job.
- **ajsutton** reopened the issue after observing 4 flakes in recent CI runs and initiated an investigation.
