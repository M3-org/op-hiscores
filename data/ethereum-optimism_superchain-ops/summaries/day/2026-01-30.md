### op-node: On the Initial EL Sync UX and Correctness ([#17631](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/superchain-ops/issues/17631))
Discussion continued regarding the `op-node`'s behavior of marking the target as finalized after initial EL Sync, even if the block is not actually finalized. A comment from `trianglesphere` clarified that this was the intended design behavior, though it could be considered a bug now. The comment also noted that re-running derivation on a snap-synced node does not work as the node can only execute blocks after snap syncing is complete.

### op-reth: batcher throttling ([#18764](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/superchain-ops/issues/18764))
The discussion focused on the implementation of batcher throttling in `op-reth` and `op-rbuilder`. `einar-oplabs` clarified that the throttling mechanism is set via JSON-RPC rather than CLI parameters. `einar-oplabs` also provided an update on the progress, noting that `op-reth` has throttle logic and a JSON-RPC interface, but tests are still in progress. `op-rbuilder` has throttle logic and tests, but needs a JSON-RPC server and endpoint.

### op-supernode: Safe Label Adjusting ([#18976](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/superchain-ops/issues/18976))
The conversation revolved around a proposed diagram and terminology for understanding chain states (EL, CL, Super) and how safe labels are managed. `axelKingsley` praised the diagram and terminology, noting that the design seems promising. `geoknee` elaborated on options for reading data from CL and EL, suggesting either adorning ELs with a "local safe" label or having the supernode communicate with the CL to determine which block to retrieve from the EL. `geoknee` also highlighted a potential deadlock if the EL's safe label is modified to not progress until cross-safety is established.
