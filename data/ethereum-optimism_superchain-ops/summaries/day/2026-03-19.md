## ACTIVE ISSUES

### Addressing CI Flakiness in Interop Proofs
Discussions continued on several flaky CI tests related to interop proofs. For [#19673](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/superchain-ops/issues/19673) (`TestProposer` hanging indefinitely), the root cause was identified as `FailNow` firing inside `testify.Eventually` goroutines, causing them to silently die and `Eventually` to block. The proposed fix involves replacing fatal assertions with error-returning variants within `Eventually` conditions. Similarly, for [#19672](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/superchain-ops/issues/19672) (`TestChallengerPlaysGame` hanging), it was determined that this test was collateral damage from `TestProposer` hanging. The fix involves isolating `TestProposer` into a sub-package to prevent cascading failures. These findings also relate to [#19563](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/superchain-ops/issues/19563), which tracks the general non-terminating `memory-all-opn-op-geth` CI job, with `TestChallengerPlaysGame` and `TestProposer` being recent culprits.

### Initial EL Sync UX and Correctness
The ongoing discussion in [#17631](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/superchain-ops/issues/17631) revolves around the correctness and user experience of the `op-node`'s initial EL sync. The core issue is that after initial EL sync, the safe and finalized heads are incorrectly set to the unsafe head, rather than reflecting the L1 finalization gadget. Option 2, proposed by @trianglesphere, suggests setting safe/finalized to the unsafe head minus the sequencer window (~12 hours). This approach aims to balance correctness with improved UX by reducing derivation time compared to starting from genesis. The latest comment from @pcw109550 reiterates the importance of correctness and outlines three options for initializing safe/finalized heads, concluding that Option 2 offers a reasonable balance.

### Kona-Interop Block Hash Oracle Review
The review of the `kona-interop` block hash oracle, tracked in [#18288](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/superchain-ops/issues/18288), continues. The current implementation uses EIP-2935 for efficient block hash lookups. The latest comments from @ajsutton and @digorithm discuss the need for an integration/acceptance test to verify block lookups prior to the current history window, specifically in the context of `op-supernode` rather than `op-supervisor`. The challenge lies in generating sufficient chain history for such a test, with suggestions to leverage `op-acceptance-test`'s time travel capabilities.
