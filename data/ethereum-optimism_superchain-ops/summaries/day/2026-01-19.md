## ACTIVE ISSUES

### Addressing `op-reth` Batcher Throttling
Discussion on [#18764](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/superchain-ops/issues/18764) highlights that `op-reth` implements both throttle mechanisms in its payload builder, originating from a specific commit, but lacks corresponding tests. In contrast, `op-rbuilder`'s payload builder includes several tests for data availability, block size limits, and transaction size limits, suggesting a more robust testing approach for throttling mechanisms.

### `op-supernode` Reset Handling Strategies
The conversation around [#18740](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/superchain-ops/issues/18740) delves into the complexities of `op-supernode`'s reset handling, particularly regarding the `engine_forkchoiceUpdatedV1` behavior. The latest plan is to initially implement a workaround by updating to a synthetic block and then back to the desired block, as demonstrated in a recent commit. Alternative long-term options include integrating supernode-driven resets with existing reorg mechanisms or introducing a new engine API method for precise control. There's also confusion about how `FCU` previously worked given that it doesn't move the unsafe head back to a canonical block on `op-geth`.

### Investigating `op-reth` <-> `op-geth` Connection Flakiness
Issue [#18570](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/superchain-ops/issues/18570) addresses flakiness in `op-reth` and `op-geth` P2P connections within system tests. A pull request related to this issue has been made, and a community member has emphasized its criticality, suggesting it be classified as a major bug or high-priority chore due to its impact on test stability and potential production compatibility. The need for someone to investigate P2P implementation differences and `admin_peers` encoding is highlighted.
