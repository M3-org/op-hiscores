# ethereum-optimism/design-docs Weekly Report (Jul 19 - 25, 2026)

## 🚀 Highlights
This week focused on significant architectural cleanup and protocol refinement, headlined by the successful completion of the `op-geth` decoupling project. By migrating call-sites to `op-core/*`, the team has streamlined the dependencies between core components like `op-node` and `op-batcher`. While infrastructure stability remains a priority, the team is also addressing complex integration flakes in the `reth-optimism-node` and refining the documentation for smart contract feature lifecycles. Notably, a sensitive technical concern regarding SDM/Kona proofs has prompted a shift toward secure, non-public coordination among core developers.

## 🛠️ Key Developments

### Protocol Architecture & Decoupling
The primary technical achievement this week was the finalization of the `op-geth` decoupling initiative. This work ensures a cleaner separation of concerns within the OP Stack by moving shared logic into centralized core packages.
*   **Core Migration:** Call-sites across `op-node`, `op-service`, and `op-batcher` were successfully swapped to `op-core/*`. The final scope of this migration was landed through [#21791](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/design-docs/pull/21791) and [#21904](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/design-docs/pull/21904).

### Documentation & Standards
Efforts are underway to improve the clarity of how features move through the Optimism ecosystem, ensuring developers and governance participants have a shared understanding of contract maturity.
*   **Feature Lifecycles:** A new pull request, [#389](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/design-docs/pull/389), was introduced to define and clarify the lifecycle stages of smart contract features.

## 🐛 Issues & Triage

### Closed Issues
*   **Op-Geth Decoupling ([#20263](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/design-docs/issues/20263)):** This major architectural issue was closed following the resolution of all sub-tasks. The completion marks a milestone in reducing the technical debt associated with tightly coupled execution engine dependencies.

### New & Active Issues
*   **Reth Integration Stability ([#20973](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/design-docs/issues/20973)):** Investigation continues into a persistent `SIGSEGV` occurring during the teardown of `reth-optimism-node` integration tests (specifically `peers_negotiate_eth_69`). While previous theories regarding the "detached payload thread" have been refuted, the team is evaluating alternative fixes in [#20983](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/design-docs/pull/20983) and [#21562](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/design-docs/pull/21562) to bypass the upstream race condition. The next step involves capturing core dumps in CI for definitive diagnosis.
*   **SDM/Kona Proof Coverage ([#21344](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/design-docs/issues/21344)):** Work on adding coverage for SDM in `rust/kona/tests/proofs` has surfaced a technical concern that requires private disclosure. This indicates a potential security or high-sensitivity edge case in the PostExec/Kona stack that is currently being triaged by core contributors.

## 💬 Community & Collaboration
The week's activity highlights deep technical collaboration between maintainers like @sebastianst and @nonsense, particularly in the rigorous debugging of the Reth integration flake. The interaction on the SDM coverage issue ([#21344](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/design-docs/issues/21344)) demonstrates the project's commitment to security, as contributors are actively seeking secure communication channels to discuss sensitive protocol vulnerabilities or logic flaws. This proactive coordination between the community and core developers remains vital for the stability of the Superchain's proof systems.
