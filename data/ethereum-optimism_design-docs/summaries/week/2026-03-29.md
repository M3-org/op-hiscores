# ethereum-optimism/design-docs Weekly Report (Mar 29 - Apr 4, 2026)

## 🚀 Highlights
This week focused on advancing the architectural framework for the Optimism protocol's evolution, specifically regarding the shared dispute game migration. Significant effort was also directed toward stabilizing the testing infrastructure for interoperability and fault proofs. Developers successfully identified and proposed fixes for several critical flaky tests that were impacting the reliability of the Superchain's CI/CD pipeline.

## 🛠️ Key Developments

### Protocol Design & Migration
The primary architectural advancement this week was the introduction of a comprehensive design document for the **shared dispute game migration**. This document outlines the necessary architectural changes and migration paths required for this significant protocol upgrade, ensuring a secure transition for the dispute resolution mechanism.
*   **Shared Dispute Game Migration:** Detailed architectural considerations for the protocol upgrade ([#370](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/design-docs/pull/370)).

### Testing Infrastructure & Stability
A major theme of the week was the systematic identification and resolution of race conditions and polling inconsistencies within the interop and fault proof testing suites.
*   **Fault Proof Test Stabilization:** Addressed inconsistencies in `TestPreinteropFaultProofs_VariedBlockTimes` by shifting from supernode polling to direct L1 head reads. This change ensures that invariants are maintained even during active sequencer and batcher choreography ([#19805](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/design-docs/pull/19805)).
*   **SafeDB Concurrency Fixes:** Proposed a fix for panics occurring during chain shutdowns in `TestFollowSource_HeadsDivergeThenConverge`. The solution introduces `ErrClosed` guards to SafeDB methods to ensure graceful error handling when goroutines attempt to access the database after the Interop service has stopped ([#19824](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/design-docs/pull/19824)).

## 🐛 Issues & Triage

### New & Active Issues
The community focused on triaging complex failures within the interoperability stack:
*   **Interoperability Fault Proofs:** Discussion on [#19804](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/design-docs/issues/19804) led to the discovery that shifting `RequiredL1` values were causing test flakiness, resulting in the aforementioned fix in PR #19805.
*   **Super Cannon Testing:** Issue [#19806](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/design-docs/issues/19806) investigated a reported failure in `TestSuperCannonRootBeyondProposedBlock`. Analysis suggests this was a false positive report, though it highlights the sensitivity of the Cannon execution environment.
*   **Shutdown Race Conditions:** Issue [#19821](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/design-docs/issues/19821) remains active as developers refine the lifecycle management of goroutines to prevent panics during the termination of chain containers.

## 💬 Community & Collaboration
Collaboration this week was characterized by deep technical root-cause analysis of protocol-level tests. Maintainers and contributors engaged in detailed debugging of the interaction between the supernode, batcher, and SafeDB components. The rapid transition from identifying flaky tests (e.g., in the Interop and Cannon suites) to proposing architectural guards demonstrates a highly responsive and security-conscious development cycle. There was also notable critical evaluation of automated tooling, as seen in the investigation of false-positive test reports.
