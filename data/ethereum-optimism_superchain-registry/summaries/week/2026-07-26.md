# ethereum-optimism/superchain-registry Weekly Report (Jul 26 - Aug 1, 2026)

## 🚀 Highlights
This week, the Superchain Registry focused on streamlining configurations and expanding support for advanced game types within the OP Stack. Key activities included the removal of deprecated `sepolia-dev-0` configurations and legacy Kona prestate versions to maintain registry hygiene. Significant progress was made on defining the upgrade path for interoperable chains, with a consensus forming around idempotent upgrades for shared components. Meanwhile, technical investigations into EL-backfill stalls and precompile gas reservations for the upcoming Glamsterdam hardfork remain top priorities for protocol stability.

## 🛠️ Key Developments

### Registry Maintenance and Cleanup
Efforts this week focused on reducing technical debt and ensuring the registry reflects the current state of the ecosystem.
*   **Chain Deprecation:** The `sepolia-dev-0` chain and all its associated generated registry entries and obsolete references were removed, streamlining the configuration set ([#1294](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/superchain-registry/pull/1294)).
*   **Prestate Validation:** Six legacy Kona prestate versions (1.1.6 through 1.1.11) were removed from `standard-prestates.toml`, ensuring that validation processes only target current, supported configurations ([#1296](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/superchain-registry/pull/1296)).

### Super-Root and Codegen Enhancements
The registry's operational tooling was updated to accommodate new proof systems and automated maintenance.
*   **Game Type Support:** The `ops` module now supports new super-root game types, specifically classifying `SUPER_PERMISSIONED` (type 5) and `SUPER_CANNON_KONA` (type 9) during codegen. This update included a temporary pin of the `optimism` dependency to ensure stability during the transition ([#1290](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/superchain-registry/pull/1290)).
*   **Automated Updates:** Routine maintenance continued with the opening of automated codegen updates ([#1295](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/superchain-registry/pull/1295)).

## 🐛 Issues & Triage

### Closed Issues
*   **Proposer Instance Detection:** Developers closed the investigation into detecting duplicate `kona-sp1-proposer` instances ([#22020](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/superchain-registry/issues/22020)). It was determined that the low probability of occurrence and minor impact did not justify the architectural complexity required for a fix.

### New & Active Issues
*   **Post-Migration OPCM Upgrades:** A critical discussion is ongoing regarding how to handle upgrades for chains in an interop set that share L1 components but have different `ProxyAdmin` contracts ([#21731](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/superchain-registry/issues/21731)). The team is leaning toward an idempotent upgrade approach (Option 1), which ensures all shared components are updated even if it results in redundant calls, avoiding the risk of partially upgraded or incompatible chain states.
*   **Glamsterdam Gas Reservations:** Debate continues over `PreimageOracle.PRECOMPILE_CALL_RESERVED_GAS` for EIP-8037 ([#19380](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/superchain-registry/issues/19380)). Current estimates suggest that the proposed values may be insufficient for whole-function completion, leading to a potential redefinition of the issue to guarantee success at the guard threshold.
*   **EL Sync Stalls:** Investigation persists into flaky tests (`TestUnsafeChainNotStalling_CLSync`) where `op-reth` followers fail to backfill blocks due to peer disconnection ([#21041](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/superchain-registry/issues/21041)). Evidence points toward an Execution Layer peer-management defect rather than CI resource contention.

## 💬 Community & Collaboration
The week was characterized by deep technical coordination between maintainers, particularly regarding the `OPContractsManager` (OPCM) upgrade logic. The collaboration between contributors like **Inphi** and others highlighted a cautious, safety-first approach to interoperability, prioritizing consistent contract states across the Superchain over operational brevity. The active management of the registry—evidenced by the swift removal of legacy prestates and dev-chains—demonstrates a commitment to providing a clean, reliable source of truth for Superchain developers.
