# ethereum-optimism/superchain-registry Weekly Report (Aug 2 - 8, 2026)

## 🚀 Highlights
This week focused on critical infrastructure updates for the Superchain, specifically targeting the validation of new contract versions on the Sepolia testnet. Significant progress was made toward the rollout of the Superchain Dependency Manager (SDM) alpha/beta devnet, alongside deep technical investigations into `op-node` synchronization failures and integration test flakiness. The team also prioritized CI efficiency by shifting toward smaller, more manageable pull requests to reduce makespan.

## 🛠️ Key Developments

### Sepolia Network & Contract Validation
The registry saw several updates to ensure compatibility with upcoming releases and testing environments on the Sepolia network.
*   **U20 Release Candidate:** Updated `standard-versions-sepolia.toml` to include `op-contracts/v8.0.0-rc.2`, ensuring proper validation for the U20 release candidate [#1298](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/superchain-registry/pull/1298).
*   **PCD E2E Testing:** Added Sepolia versions for `op-contracts/v8.0.0-pcdtest` to the standard versions validation suite to support end-to-end testing for Proof of Concept deployments [#1302](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/superchain-registry/pull/1302).

### Superchain Dependency Manager (SDM) Progress
Work advanced on the SDM devnet (`sdm-net-0`), focusing on performance benchmarking and infrastructure readiness.
*   **Devnet Rollout:** The network specification has been merged, and creation workflows are nearing completion.
*   **Benchmarking:** The team is preparing to use the `op-benchmark` tool to establish baselines. Key remaining tasks include implementing contract/transaction load generation and porting the SDM Grafana dashboard [#20176](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/superchain-registry/issues/20176), [#21273](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/superchain-registry/issues/21273).

### Automated Registry Maintenance
Routine automated updates were performed to maintain the integrity of the registry's data.
*   **Address Codegen:** Multiple automated updates were merged to modify `addresses.json` within the `superchain/extra` directory, keeping the registry synchronized with deployed infrastructure [#1299](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/superchain-registry/pull/1299), [#1301](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/superchain-registry/pull/1301).

## 🐛 Issues & Triage

### Closed Issues
*   **CI Optimization:** The team resolved concerns regarding CI makespan by adopting a strategy of merging smaller, individual PRs rather than large batches [#21186](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/superchain-registry/issues/21186).
*   **op-reth Stability:** An issue involving permanent lag in `op-reth` proofs history following a clean shutdown was successfully closed [#22089](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/superchain-registry/issues/22089).

### New & Active Issues
*   **op-node Sync Failures:** Investigation continues into `op-node` failing to sync when using the `execution-layer` sync mode with an `op-reth` backend. Reports indicate persistent "ecies auth failed" errors, though some pods eventually establish peers after repeated retries [#20974](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/superchain-registry/issues/20974).
*   **Integration Test Flakiness:** A SIGSEGV crash in `reth-optimism-node` integration tests during teardown remains under investigation. While initial hypotheses regarding the payload thread were refuted, the team is now looking into unclosed MDBX environments or background RocksDB threads as potential causes. CI has been instrumented to capture core dumps for further analysis [#20973](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/superchain-registry/issues/20973).

## 💬 Community & Collaboration
The week was characterized by high-level technical troubleshooting between core contributors. Notable collaboration occurred between `jlynch1` and `sebastianst` regarding the `op-node` and `reth` synchronization issues. The discussion around the SDM devnet rollout also showed tight coordination between the tooling and core protocol teams to align on load generation and "hot-toggle" policy mechanisms. The shift in CI strategy reflects a community-wide effort to improve the developer experience by reducing wait times for PR validation.
