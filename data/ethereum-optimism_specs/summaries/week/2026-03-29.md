# ethereum-optimism/specs Weekly Report (Mar 29 - 4, 2026)

## 🚀 Highlights
This week, development focused on refining the specifications for the upcoming **Jovian release**, specifically regarding L1Block hydration and scalar synchronization. The project saw a significant improvement in CI stability as several long-standing flaky tests related to database preservation and proposer logic were closed after consistent passing streaks. However, new challenges emerged within the Interop Fault Proofs suite, where discrepancies in the Kona interop program's exit states have identified a need for more robust L1 head checks in the transition path.

## 🛠️ Key Developments

### Jovian Release Specification Sync
Efforts were concentrated on aligning the documentation with the latest implementation details for the Jovian upgrade. This work ensures that the protocol's foundational data structures are accurately represented for developers and integrators.
*   **L1Block Hydration & Scalars:** Updates were proposed to the documentation to reflect new methods for L1Block hydration and updated scalars specific to Jovian ([#899](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/specs/pull/899)).
*   **Link Maintenance:** A synchronization effort was completed to repair broken specification links and ensure consistency across the Jovian L1Block method documentation ([#898](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/specs/pull/898)).

## 🐛 Issues & Triage

### Closed Issues: CI Stability & Flake Resolution
The repository saw a major cleanup of flaky tests that had previously hindered the CI pipeline. These were closed following high volumes of consecutive successful passes on the `develop` branch.
*   **Database & Sync Integrity:** Tests covering database preservation during Consensus Layer (CL) resyncs and truncation during Execution Layer (EL) resyncs were resolved after over 70 successful runs ([#19661](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/specs/issues/19661)).
*   **Proposer & Interop Logic:** Issues involving the `TestProposer` hanging and invalid message replacement in supernode interop were closed after demonstrating stability ([#19673](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/specs/issues/19673), [#19677](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/specs/issues/19677)).
*   **Diagnostic Cleanup:** A non-actionable CI flake in the bedrock upgrade tests was closed due to a lack of diagnostic data and recent reports ([#19577](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/specs/issues/19577)).

### New & Active Issues: Interop Fault Proofs
While old flakes were resolved, new issues surfaced regarding the **Kona interop program** within the fault proof testing suite.
*   **Kona Transition Errors:** Two sibling issues ([#19829](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/specs/issues/19829), [#19828](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/specs/issues/19828)) highlight cases where the Kona program returns incorrect validity signals (e.g., exiting as valid when a failure is expected, or vice versa). 
*   **Root Cause Analysis:** Initial triage suggests these failures stem from a missing L1 head check in the Kona "no-op" transition path, which is currently being investigated as a priority for interop stability.

## 💬 Community & Collaboration
The week was characterized by a strong focus on "test hygiene" and specification accuracy. The closure of multiple long-standing issues (some with over 70 consecutive passes) indicates a disciplined approach by maintainers to monitor and validate CI health over time. Collaboration is currently centered on the Interop Fault Proofs, where contributors are actively diagnosing the Kona program's behavior to ensure the Superchain's security model remains robust across varied block times.
