# ethereum-optimism/specs Weekly Report (Jan 25 - 31, 2026)

## 🚀 Highlights
This week, development focused heavily on formalizing the **L2 contract upgrade specifications**, marking a significant step toward standardizing how the Superchain manages protocol evolutions. A comprehensive suite of new specifications was introduced to detail the upgrade process, including the introduction of the `L2ContractsManager` and `L2ProxyAdmin`. Beyond upgrades, the team reached a consensus on the interface requirements for the `SyncStatus` API to support interop activity and began addressing system discrepancies and "permanent rights" assessments. While some interoperability tasks remain on hold pending design rollouts, the week established a clear technical framework for more secure and predictable network transitions.

## 🛠️ Key Developments

### L2 Contract Upgrade Framework
The primary technical focus was the creation of a robust specification for L2 contract upgrades. This work aims to provide a clear roadmap for how the protocol handles state and logic transitions across the ecosystem.
*   **Upgrade Process & Logic:** New specifications were introduced to define the complete Upgrade Process ([#881](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/specs/pull/881)) and the high-level overview of L2 contract upgrades, including the `ConditionalDeployer` ([#876](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/specs/pull/876)).
*   **Administrative Components:** Detailed specs were added for the `L2ProxyAdmin` ([#877](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/specs/pull/877)) and the `L2ContractsManager` ([#878](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/specs/pull/878)), which are critical for managing the lifecycle of system contracts.
*   **Transaction & Resource Management:** Work began on specifying the Network Upgrade Transaction Bundle ([#879](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/specs/pull/879)) and implementing a Custom Upgrade Block Gas Limit ([#880](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/specs/pull/880)) to ensure upgrades do not exceed network constraints.
*   **General Specifications:** Additional L2 contract upgrade specifications were consolidated under [#884](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/specs/pull/884).

## 🐛 Issues & Triage

### Closed Issues
*   **Upgrade Process Refinements:** Several legacy issues were resolved as the specification evolved. [#18536](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/specs/issues/18536) was closed following the merger of the CGT PR, and [#18633](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/specs/issues/18633) was closed after the deletion of a redundant Resource Locator for v7_0_0 artifacts.

### New & Active Issues
*   **System Assessment:** New investigations were launched into "permanent rights" ([#882](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/specs/issues/882)) and discrepancies between "wiz sensors" in autopilot versus standard configurations ([#883](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/specs/issues/883)).
*   **SyncStatus API:** A consensus was reached on [#18947](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/specs/issues/18947) regarding the `op-supernode` SyncStatus API. The interface will now include `op-node` status for every chain and verification timestamps to simplify acceptance testing for interoperability.
*   **Withdrawal Mechanisms:** Discussion on [#18517](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/specs/issues/18517) led to moving chain-specific root claim proving to the backlog. The team decided that `OptimismPortal2` upgrades must be decoupled from `FaultDisputeGame` changes to prevent user withdrawal delays.
*   **Blockers:** The Super DA Error specification ([#725](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/specs/issues/725)) remains "on ice" pending the rollout of a new interoperability design.

## 💬 Community & Collaboration
The week demonstrated strong community engagement, particularly in the `op-alloy` ecosystem. A community member stepped forward to address EIP-1559 parameter decoding ([#18951](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/specs/issues/18951)), specifically targeting zero elasticities for earlier failure detection. Collaboration between maintainers was evident in the rapid iteration of the upgrade specifications and the technical consensus reached on the SyncStatus API, ensuring that the `op-supernode` requirements align with the needs of multi-chain verification.
