# ethereum-optimism/specs Weekly Report (Aug 30 - Sep 5, 2026)

## 🚀 Highlights
This week, the protocol specifications saw a major pivot with the formal replacement of the Flashblocks specification in favor of a more streamlined **Subblocks** framework. This transition focuses on providing a minimal, consumer-facing interface while ensuring robust sequencing guarantees and JSON-RPC behavior. Significant progress was also made in infrastructure stability, marked by the successful redeployment of light nodes on staging and the resolution of critical inefficiencies in the game-proving retry mechanism.

## 🛠️ Key Developments

### Protocol Evolution: Subblocks Integration
The primary technical focus this week was the refinement of the Subblocks specification, which replaces the previous Flashblocks proposal. This shift aims to simplify the integration for downstream consumers while maintaining high-performance sequencing.
*   **Specification Refinement:** The team documented the WebSocket payload format, sequencing guarantees, and transaction inclusion implications for Subblocks ([#933](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/specs/pull/933)).
*   **Flashblocks Deprecation:** The transition was finalized by closing the tracking issue for the Flashblocks replacement, signaling a move toward a more minimal protocol design ([#929](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/specs/issues/929)).

### Portal & Security Enhancements
Work began on refining the `optimism-portal` to improve the management of withdrawal states.
*   **Withdrawal Management:** A new pull request was introduced to specify the `deleteProvenWithdrawal` function, enhancing the protocol's ability to handle specific withdrawal lifecycle events ([#936](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/specs/pull/936)).

## 🐛 Issues & Triage

### Closed Issues
*   **Infrastructure & Testing:** The light node deployment across the Staging environment was successfully completed, ensuring that testing infrastructure remains at parity with current development ([#22352](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/specs/issues/22352)).
*   **Proving Efficiency:** A resolution was reached regarding the game-proving retry mechanism. Previously, retries were inefficiently discarding completed proofs and regenerating all witnesses; this fix streamlines the proving pipeline ([#22622](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/specs/issues/22622)).

### New & Active Issues
*   **Kona-node Performance:** A significant active discussion persists regarding the `NetworkActor` in `kona-node` stalling the P2P swarm during remote-signer HTTP calls. The team is currently evaluating the impact of injected latency on inbound payload handling and peer scoring to prevent network-level bottlenecks ([#22646](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/specs/issues/22646)).

## 💬 Community & Collaboration
Collaboration this week was characterized by a focus on design parity and incremental development. In the `kona-node` discussions, contributors @geoknee and @jdodinh emphasized the importance of smaller, manageable PRs to address complex P2P networking issues. @jdodinh has stepped forward to lead the benchmarking efforts for the `NetworkActor` stalling issue, demonstrating active community ownership of critical performance bottlenecks.
