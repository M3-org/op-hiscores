# ethereum-optimism/specs Weekly Report (Jul 19 - 25, 2026)

## 🚀 Highlights
This week focused on advancing protocol specifications and refining deployment workflows for the Superchain. Key activities included the introduction of EIP-8130 into the specification suite and the resolution of critical connectivity bugs in `op-reth`. A significant portion of the week's effort was dedicated to addressing technical debt in the deployment pipeline, specifically regarding the integration of real anchor roots for permissionless interoperability and OPCM V2.

## 🛠️ Protocol & Specification Developments
The project continues to evolve its technical standards to ensure alignment with the broader Ethereum ecosystem and the specific needs of the OP Stack.

### Specification Updates
- **EIP-8130 Integration:** A new pull request, [#924](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/specs/pull/924), was opened to incorporate the EIP-8130 specification. This addition represents a continued effort to keep the Optimism specs synchronized with emerging Ethereum Improvement Proposals.

### Infrastructure & Tooling Fixes
- **`op-reth` Connectivity:** Developers resolved a significant TLS connection error affecting `op-reth` v1.11.0. The issue previously prevented stable WebSocket connections to flashblocks endpoints, and the fix has been synchronized with the main `optimism` repository.

## 🐛 Issues & Triage
The week saw a mix of successful bug resolution and the identification of persistent technical debt within the deployment architecture.

- **Closed Issues:** The primary resolution this week was the fix for [#19322](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/specs/issues/19322), which addressed the aforementioned TLS issues in `op-reth`.
- **Active & Reopened Issues:** Issue [#20912](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/specs/issues/20912), concerning the addition of starting anchor roots and prestate hashes to deploy inputs, was reopened. Automated CI checks identified outstanding TODOs in the codebase, signaling that the deploy pipelines are still utilizing placeholder data. This remains a priority for ensuring the security and validity of permissionless `interopgen` and OPCM V2 initial deployments.

## 💬 Community & Collaboration
Collaboration this week was characterized by tight integration between specification tracking and codebase implementation. The reopening of the anchor root issue by @claude highlights an active feedback loop between CI monitoring and protocol design. This ensures that architectural requirements—such as the transition from placeholder to real anchor roots—are not overlooked during the rapid development of the Superchain's interoperability layers.
