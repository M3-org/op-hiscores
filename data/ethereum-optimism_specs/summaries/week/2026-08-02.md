# ethereum-optimism/specs Weekly Report (Aug 2 - 8, 2026)

## 🚀 Highlights
This week focused on refining the Lagoon protocol upgrade, specifically targeting interoperability and data availability (DA) efficiency. Significant progress was made in streamlining the Superchain's transaction specifications by adopting the NUT bundle and optimizing DA footprint accounting. Meanwhile, the team addressed critical infrastructure stability, focusing on CI makespan reductions and investigating persistent synchronization and crash issues within the `op-node` and `op-reth` environments.

## 🛠️ Key Developments

### Lagoon Protocol & Interoperability
The Lagoon upgrade remains a primary focus, with updates aimed at improving both cross-chain capabilities and resource accounting.
*   **NUT Bundle Integration:** A major proposal was introduced to replace the superseded Lagoon upgrade-transaction specification with the NUT bundle. This shift is designed to enhance interoperability capabilities across the Superchain ecosystem ([#927](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/specs/pull/927)).
*   **DA Footprint Optimization:** The team refined the Lagoon specification by excluding post-execution transactions (`0x7D`) from Jovian Data Availability accounting. By specifying zero contribution to `daFootprint` and `blobGasUsed`, the protocol now provides clearer accounting for late-constructed transactions and addresses linear underestimation issues ([#926](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/specs/pull/926)).

### Infrastructure & CI Improvements
*   **CI Makespan Reduction:** To improve developer velocity, the team closed efforts to lower CI makespan by adopting a strategy of merging smaller, individual PRs rather than large, monolithic batches ([#21186](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/specs/issues/21186)).

## 🐛 Issues & Triage

### Closed Issues
*   **Proofs History Consistency:** The issue regarding `op-reth` proofs history lagging after a clean shutdown was resolved. The consensus reached was to ensure database consistency on startup rather than modifying shutdown logic, ensuring the proofs execution extension (exex) catches up as the chain produces blocks ([#22089](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/specs/issues/22089)).

### New & Active Issues
*   **SDM Devnet Rollout:** Significant discussion surrounded the `sdm-net-0` Sepolia alphanet. While Terraform and L2 contract deployments are complete, work remains on contract/transaction load generation and porting the SDM Grafana dashboard for baseline benchmarking ([#21273](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/specs/issues/21273), [#20176](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/specs/issues/20176)).
*   **Sync & Stability Blockers:** 
    *   `op-node` continues to face peering difficulties when using the `execution-layer` sync mode, characterized by `ECIES` errors and stream failures ([#20974](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/specs/issues/20974)).
    *   The `reth-optimism-node` integration tests are plagued by intermittent `SIGSEGV` crashes during teardown. Current efforts involve instrumenting CI to capture core dumps to distinguish between potential MDBX, RocksDB, or threading conflicts ([#20973](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/specs/issues/20973)).

## 💬 Community & Collaboration
Collaboration this week was centered on deep technical troubleshooting between protocol researchers and infrastructure engineers. The discussion on issue [#22089](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/specs/issues/22089) showcased a collaborative effort to define robust state-consistency patterns for `op-reth`. Additionally, the community is actively engaged in the SDM (Superchain Dependency Manager) rollout, with contributors coordinating on benchmarking SOPs and load-generation scripts to ensure the new network's stability.
