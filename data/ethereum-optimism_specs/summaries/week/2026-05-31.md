# ethereum-optimism/specs Weekly Report (May 31 - June 6, 2026)

## 🚀 Highlights
This week focused on the formalization of the Osaka hardfork specifications and the refinement of interoperability recovery procedures. Significant progress was made in finalizing the Osaka L2 draft specifications and integrating Karst Osaka changes into the protocol overview. Meanwhile, the team addressed critical operational challenges regarding interop failsafes and transaction pool recovery, while continuing to synchronize core dependencies like `op-revm` with the broader Ethereum ecosystem.

## 🛠️ Key Developments

### Osaka Hardfork & Protocol Specifications
The project reached a milestone in defining the next phase of the protocol.
*   **Osaka L2 Finalization:** The draft specifications for Osaka on Layer 2 have been finalized, marking the completion of the initial design phase ([#903](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/specs/issues/903)).
*   **Karst Osaka Integration:** Updates were merged into the protocol specifications to include Karst Osaka changes, specifically updating the `SUMMARY.md` and `overview.md` files within the `specs/protocol/karst` directory ([#904](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/specs/pull/904)).
*   **Safer Safes:** A new specification was introduced for "Safer Safes" contracts, aiming to enhance security standards for smart contract wallets within the ecosystem ([#914](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/specs/pull/914)).

### Infrastructure & Tooling
Efforts to improve the developer experience and local testing environments continued.
*   **Devnet Evolution:** Progress was reported on local devnet stability. The team is currently transitioning from local instances to establishing a long-running devnet, pending the availability of `glamsterdam-devnet-4` images ([#20691](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/specs/issues/20691)).
*   **Dependency Synchronization:** Discussions finalized a strategy for `op-revm` to stay in lockstep with `revm` releases. This includes adopting `revm` v111 to leverage Amsterdam hardfork support and shared type definitions, ensuring Optimism remains compatible with the latest upstream improvements ([#21069](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/specs/issues/21069)).

## 🐛 Issues & Triage

### Closed Issues
*   **Specification Drafting:** The primary focus of closed work was the finalization of the Osaka L2 specs ([#903](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/specs/issues/903)), transitioning the project from the drafting phase to implementation readiness.

### New & Active Issues
*   **Interoperability Recovery:** A critical new issue was opened to develop a "runbook" for chain recovery following reorgs triggered by invalid interop messages. This highlights a need for more robust automated recovery tools ([#21161](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/specs/issues/21161)).
*   **Transaction Pool Failsafes:** A bug was identified in `op-reth` where the interop transaction pool fails to resume after a failsafe is disabled without a full manual restart of both the sequencer and builder. To mitigate this in the future, a new failsafe-state gauge metric has been proposed to improve observability ([#21149](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/specs/issues/21149)).

## 💬 Community & Collaboration
Collaboration this week was characterized by tight coordination between protocol researchers and engine developers. Key contributors including **joshklop**, **Wodann**, **sebastianst**, and **jelias2** were active in bridging the gap between high-level specifications and implementation details. The dialogue regarding `op-revm` synchronization demonstrates a proactive commitment to maintaining upstream compatibility, while the troubleshooting of the interop transaction pool shows a high level of transparency in resolving sequencer-level operational hurdles.
