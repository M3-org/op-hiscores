# ethereum-optimism/specs Weekly Report (Jun 21 - 27, 2026)

## 🚀 Highlights
This week, the Optimism specs repository saw a significant shift toward integrating Proof-Carrying Data (PCD) into the protocol architecture. Key milestones included the finalization of the PCD infrastructure design and the introduction of initial specifications and documentation for this new feature. Parallel to these architectural advancements, the team focused on protocol hardening for the Superchain Dependency Manager (SDM) and addressed critical gas estimation discrepancies on the Sepolia testnet following the Karst hard fork.

## 🛠️ Key Developments

### Proof-Carrying Data (PCD) Integration
The project has officially moved into the implementation phase for PCD, a critical step for enhancing the scalability and verification capabilities of the stack.
*   **Specifications & Documentation:** Initial technical specifications for PCD were introduced in [#921](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/specs/pull/921), supported by a dedicated documentation PR [#920](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/specs/pull/920) to guide implementers.
*   **Infrastructure Design:** The foundational design document for PCD infrastructure support was finalized and approved, marking the transition from conceptual design to active development ([#21327](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/specs/issues/21327)).

### Protocol Hardening & Refinement
Efforts continued to ensure the robustness of the Superchain and the efficiency of the codebase.
*   **SDM Test Hardening:** Significant work was completed to harden tests for the Superchain Dependency Manager (SDM). This included implementing checks for ETH supply conservation, handling `PostExecSettlementUnderflow` scenarios, and ensuring the rejection of out-of-order index payloads ([#21324](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/specs/issues/21324)).
*   **Codebase Optimization:** A new initiative was launched to reduce code duplication by reusing upstream `slot-preimage` seeding helpers within `op-reth`, streamlining the integration between the execution client and the rollup node ([#21523](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/specs/issues/21523)).

## 🐛 Issues & Triage

### Closed Issues
*   **Infrastructure & Testing:** The team successfully closed the design phase for PCD infrastructure ([#21327](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/specs/issues/21327)) and completed a comprehensive hardening of the SDM test suite ([#21324](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/specs/issues/21324)), ensuring better protocol stability.

### New & Active Issues
*   **Kona-node SafeDB Implementation:** Development is underway for `SafeDB` within the `kona-node` context. Current discussions involve the creation of a `SafeDBv1` trait and the potential for a `SafeDBv2` to better support interoperability and proofs compared to the legacy `op-node` design ([#20278](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/specs/issues/20278)).
*   **Sepolia Gas Estimation Bug:** A high-priority issue persists on Optimism Sepolia where `eth_estimateGas` incorrectly returns "intrinsic gas too high" errors following the Karst hard fork ([#21457](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/specs/issues/21457)). While manual gas limits allow transactions to proceed, a formal fix is slated for the next release.

## 💬 Community & Collaboration
The week was characterized by active technical coordination between OPLabs contributors and the broader ecosystem. Notable collaboration occurred in the `kona-node` development, where contributors `einar-oplabs` and `geoknee` are iterating on database traits to ensure compatibility with future interop requirements. Additionally, community reporting from users and external tool providers like `Tenderly` proved vital in diagnosing the Karst-related gas estimation issues, demonstrating a healthy feedback loop between protocol developers and infrastructure providers.
