## ACTIVE ISSUES

### Kona Engine: Targeted Reset Implementation
Discussion on [#22526](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/specs/issues/22526) (kona-engine: targeted reset) indicates that the feature has been implemented in #22586. The latest comment from claude details the considerations for handling the cross-safe head during resets, emphasizing that resets never move the cross-safe head forward and that the invariant is maintained in the state transition rather than solely in the reset function. It also notes a deliberate difference in retry ceilings between `reset` and `reset_to`.

### Kona Engine: Local/Cross-Safe Vocabulary and Promotion Entry Point
Issue [#22525](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/specs/issues/22525) (kona-engine: local/cross-safe vocabulary and single cross-safe promotion entry point) has seen progress with the opening of #22576. claude's comment highlights that the structural seal for `cross_safe_head` is enforced at the crate boundary, ensuring it's only written through a single promotion entry point. It also discusses the re-pointing of local-safe and cross-safe heads for different functionalities and notes a discrepancy between `cargo test` and `cargo nextest` for this crate.

### Kona: Chain_ID Metric Labeling
The discussion on [#22529](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/specs/issues/22529) (kona: chain_id metric label) indicates implementation in #22569. claude's comment details challenges with the macro surface for variadic labels, leading to a change in the order of arguments for `set!` and `record!`. It also addresses a pre-existing issue with unit tests for metrics and clarifies the scope of the implementation to `kona-engine` only, noting that a full sweep across all crates would be a larger effort.

### Lokahi: Interop Actor, Cross-Safe Happy Path (Phase 3)
Issue [#22522](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/specs/issues/22522) (lokahi: interop actor, cross-safe happy path (phase 3)) has new comments indexing decisions from a Slack design discussion. These decisions cover retention and storage engine, WAL-replay hazard for the invalidated-output archive, and a divergence in activation sources between Kona and op-supernode.

### Kona-Node-Service: Multi-Chain L1 Watcher
Discussion on [#22531](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/specs/issues/22531) (kona-node-service: multi-chain L1 watcher) indicates implementation in #22567. claude's comment details how `tokio::select!` shaped the refactor, requiring event handling after the `select!` expression to avoid borrowing collisions. It also explains the decision to keep one `eth_getLogs` per chain and notes that chain labels are intentionally omitted from log lines for now. A correction was added, stating that the missing plan file mentioned in the initial notes is out of scope.

### Lokahi: N-Chain Service Composition and Config
On [#22532](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/specs/issues/22532) (lokahi: N-chain service composition and config), a decision from @ajsutton confirms that the L1 watcher's fixed, non-empty chain set is correct by design. This means the watcher does not need to support dynamic chain attachment or a zero-chain configuration, as the CLI should enforce a non-empty chain list at startup.

### Kona-Interop: Consolidate Shared Message-Validity Rules
Issue [#22534](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/specs/issues/22534) (kona-interop: consolidate shared message-validity rules) has seen significant discussion. @sebastianst noted that the type-3 access-list checksum computation is missing from `kona-interop` and is needed for Lokahi's verifier. claude's comment clarifies that the checksum is an implementation detail of the log store, not a consensus-protocol check, and thus belongs in Lokahi's interop store work (#22535). Another decision was made to surface the shared interop rules as a `MessageRules` type rather than free functions. claude's initial detailed enumeration of pre-existing rule implementations and Rust↔Go divergences was also corrected regarding the missing plan file.

### Lokahi-Interop: Stores (VerifiedDB + WAL, LogsDBs, Invalidated-Output Archive)
Discussion on [#22535](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/specs/issues/22535) (lokahi-interop: stores) includes a decision from Adrian Sutton regarding retention: superroots must be returnable over the full fault dispute window, with unbounded retention as a likely default. This leads to using RocksDB for stores and keeping the logs DB behind a trait. A correction to the brief clarifies that op-supernode moved to a raft-wal implementation, not a custom logsdb. Analysis of "O(1) Contains" properties and sizing estimates for different stores are also provided.

### Lokahi-Interop: Verification Round Loop
On [#22536](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/specs/issues/22536) (lokahi-interop: verification round loop), a decision from @ajsutton defers the activation-timestamp override, stating it's not required for the initial release. claude's comment confirms that Kona's per-chain fork check should be ported as-is, as op-supernode's cluster-wide activation timestamp and Kona's per-chain check compute the same thing due to startup invariants.

### Lokahi-Interop: Invalidate Decision Path
Issue [#22540](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/specs/issues/22540) (lokahi-interop: Invalidate decision path) has a detailed comment from claude highlighting a hazard: the archive write must be driven by the interop actor from the WAL, independently of which branch replay takes. It also clarifies that the archive cannot borrow the deny list's fail-open handling and that no cross-store atomicity with the replacement is required.

### Lokahi: Supernode_SyncStatus and Superroot_AtTimestamp RPCs
Discussion on [#22544](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/specs/issues/22544) (lokahi: supernode_syncStatus and superroot_atTimestamp RPCs) includes a note from Adrian Sutton that superroots must be answerable over the full fault-dispute window. claude's comment emphasizes that the invalidated-output archive needs a durability and backup story over this same window, as a missing entry would lead to incorrect superroot values being returned to op-proposer and op-challenger.
