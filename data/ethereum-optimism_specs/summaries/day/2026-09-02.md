## ACTIVE ISSUES

### Addressing `op-core/superchain` Module Consumability
Discussion continued on the issue of `op-core/superchain` being unconsumable by downstream Go modules due to `superchain-configs.zip` being gitignored ([#22678](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/specs/issues/22678)).

- **Current Status and Impact:** The `superchain-configs.zip` remains gitignored and is absent from published Go modules, preventing genuine registry consumers like `op-deployer`, `op-conductor`, and `op-challenger` from building. This is actively blocking two service modules in other OP Labs repositories.
- **Clarification on Recent PRs:** While PRs [#22700](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/optimism/pull/22700) and [#22709](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/optimism/pull/22709) reduced the number of affected packages, they did not resolve the root cause. These PRs primarily removed accidental consumers that did not genuinely need registry data.
- **Supersim Unblocked:** It was clarified that `supersim` is no longer blocked by this issue, as its path to the bundle was removed by the reachability work in [#22709](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/optimism/pull/22709).
- **Git LFS Not a Solution:** `git-lfs` is not a viable option for shipping the zip due to `cmd/go` and `proxy.golang.org` limitations. Committing the zip plainly is the only in-repo solution.
- **Cost Analysis of Committing the Zip:** Historical data from `op-geth` suggests that committing the zip would incur a marginal cost of approximately 245 KB per bump, totaling around 3 MB/year, which is significantly less than previously estimated concerns about `.git` growth.
- **Reducing Bundle Reach:** Efforts are underway to reduce the number of packages that accidentally depend on the bundle, through refactoring and dead code removal, which is orthogonal to the decision of how the zip is shipped.
