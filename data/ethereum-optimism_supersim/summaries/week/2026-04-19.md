# ethereum-optimism/supersim Weekly Report (Apr 19 - 25, 2026)

## 🚀 Highlights
This week, the `supersim` project focused heavily on refining interoperability components and stabilizing the CI environment. Significant progress was made on Interop Phase 1, with Kona proof work nearing completion and the finalization of Interop Specs. However, the team is navigating challenges with test flakiness in `CrossSafe` promotion and flashblocks, alongside resource-related OOM errors in the Rust test suite. While core contract work for Interop Phase 2 remains on track, some secondary tasks like batcher verification and `op-conductor` reorg recovery are currently flagged as "at risk" due to prioritized sync issues on devnets.

## 🛠️ Key Developments

### Interop Infrastructure & Dependency Management
The project continues to align its technical stack with the evolving Superchain specifications.
*   **Dependency Upgrades:** A pull request was opened to upgrade `axios` to version 3.0.0 ([#420](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/supersim/pull/420)), aimed at resolving a specific security or functional finding.
*   **Specification Finalization:** The Interop Specs ([#905](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/supersim/issues/905)) have been officially updated and closed, signaling a stable baseline for upcoming implementation work.

### Progress on Interop Phases
*   **Phase 1 (SuperDisputeGame):** Work is concentrated on Kona proofs, which represent the largest remaining milestone for this phase. While contract work is finished, full completion is expected within the next 1-2 weeks ([#19690](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/supersim/issues/19690)).
*   **Phase 2 (Shared Dispute Game):** This phase remains on schedule with the primary contract PRs already merged and only minor tasks remaining ([#19691](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/supersim/issues/19691)).

## 🐛 Issues & Triage

### Closed Issues
*   **Kona-Interop Validation:** Internal block access reviews concluded that Kona is correctly handling interop validation ([#19266](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/supersim/issues/19266)).
*   **Conductor & CI Stability:** Issues regarding `op-conductor` reorg support ([#20214](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/supersim/issues/20214)) and OOM errors during `reth-optimism-node` compilation in CI ([#19935](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/supersim/issues/19935)) were successfully resolved.

### New & Active Issues
*   **Regression Guarding:** A new issue was opened to implement a regression guard for Execution Layer (EL) restart recovery in `op-acceptance-tests` ([#20307](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/supersim/issues/20307)). This addresses a specific stall when the EL returns a `SYNCING` status.
*   **Persistent Test Flakiness:** 
    *   `TestTruncateDatabaseOnELResync` ([#19897](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/supersim/issues/19897)) was reopened after failures suggested a bug where `CrossSafe` fails to advance past genesis during EL Sync.
    *   `TestFlashblocksTransfer` ([#19934](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/supersim/issues/19934)) remains problematic, recently triggering a nil channel panic despite previous attempts to fix it.
*   **At-Risk Tasks:** Both the "Verification-Aware CurrentL1" for the Batcher ([#19944](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/supersim/issues/19944)) and graceful reorg recovery for `op-conductor` ([#20006](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/supersim/issues/20006)) are currently queued behind urgent devnet sync issues, jeopardizing their end-of-month completion targets.

## 💬 Community & Collaboration
The development activity this week reflects a high degree of coordination around the Interop roadmap. The reopening of flaky test issues like [#19897](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/supersim/issues/19897) and [#19934](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/supersim/issues/19934) indicates a rigorous approach to CI health, with contributors actively monitoring failure logs to distinguish between environmental flakes and genuine protocol bugs. The prioritization of devnet sync issues over secondary features demonstrates a collaborative focus on protocol stability and real-world performance.
