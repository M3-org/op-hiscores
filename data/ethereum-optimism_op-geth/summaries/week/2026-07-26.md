# ethereum-optimism/op-geth Weekly Report (Jul 26 - Aug 1, 2026)

## 🚀 Highlights
This week focused on a major cleanup of the project’s architectural roadmap and core protocol refinements. A significant technical update was implemented to integrate the 'bal' field into block header and hash calculations, impacting core validation components. Simultaneously, the team executed a massive triage of the backlog, closing dozens of issues related to the deprecated `op-program` and the superseded "Standard L2 Genesis" initiative. This shift signals a transition toward the Rust-based `kona-client` and updated deployment workflows via the Optimism Contracts Manager (OPCM).

## 🛠️ Key Developments

### Core Protocol & Block Validation
The primary technical advancement this week involved updates to the fundamental structure of the blockchain's data.
*   **Block Header and Hash Calculation Update:** The 'bal' field has been integrated into the block header and hash calculation process. This change affects core types, genesis configurations, and the beacon engine components, directly impacting how blocks are structured and validated within the OP Stack ([#804](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/op-geth/pull/804)).

### Infrastructure Restructuring
Several structural changes were finalized to streamline the developer experience and repository organization.
*   **Deployment Tooling Separation:** The effort to decouple `op-deployer` from `op-chain-ops` was successfully completed, finalizing a significant restructuring of the deployment stack ([#11925](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/op-geth/issues/11925)).
*   **OPCM Integration:** The Optimism Contracts Manager (OPCM) saw progress with the addition of permissionless games to its deployment methods ([#14235](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/op-geth/issues/14235)).

## 🐛 Issues & Triage
The week was characterized by an extensive "housekeeping" phase, where many legacy or superseded initiatives were officially closed to reflect the current technical direction.

### Closed Issues
*   **Deprecation of `op-program`:** A large suite of issues (including [#15944](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/op-geth/issues/15944), [#15965](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/op-geth/issues/15965), and [#11548](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/op-geth/issues/11548)) were closed as "not planned." This follows the removal of the Go-based `op-program` from the monorepo in favor of the Rust-based `kona-client`.
*   **Standard L2 Genesis Initiative:** The initiative to simplify L2 Genesis was superseded by newer architectural patterns. Consequently, issues related to `FeeVault` modifications ([#12297](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/op-geth/issues/12297)), `OptimismPortal` changes ([#12298](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/op-geth/issues/12298)), and L2 Genesis file generation ([#12708](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/op-geth/issues/12708)) were closed.
*   **Legacy Contract Maintenance:** Several issues regarding the `FaultDisputeGame` ([#11323](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/op-geth/issues/11323)) and various predeploy modifications ([#12303](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/op-geth/issues/12303), [#12304](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/op-geth/issues/12304)) were marked as completed or superseded by previous pull requests.

### New & Active Issues
*   **Kona Development:** While many Go-centric issues were closed, work is shifting toward the Rust ecosystem, evidenced by the superseding of the safe head DB issue in Kona ([#18748](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/op-geth/issues/18748)) with newer tracking tasks.

## 💬 Community & Collaboration
The activity this week reflects a high level of internal coordination aimed at reducing technical debt and clarifying the project's scope for external contributors. By closing out nearly 40 legacy issues, the maintainers have significantly reduced "noise" in the repository. The transition from `op-program` to `kona-client` represents a major pivot in the technical stack that will require ongoing collaboration between the Go and Rust development teams within the Superchain ecosystem. Additionally, the implementation of the `M-needs-triage` label ([#13647](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/op-geth/issues/13647)) suggests a commitment to more disciplined issue management moving forward.
