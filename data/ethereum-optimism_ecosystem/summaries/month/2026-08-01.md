# ethereum-optimism/ecosystem Monthly Report (August 2026)

## 🚀 Highlights
August was characterized by a strategic shift toward refining the developer experience through a comprehensive cleanup of the Interop ecosystem. The team focused on validating performance benchmarks for State Diff Management (SDM) and streamlining public repositories to reduce technical debt. A major initiative was launched to consolidate fragmented tools and documentation, ensuring the OP Stack remains accessible and maintainable as the Superchain scales. While core infrastructure showed stability in benchmarking, active troubleshooting continues for RPC blocking issues within the Interop filtering layer.

## 🛠️ Key Developments
While no new features were merged during this specific reporting window, the technical focus remained on infrastructure verification and architectural planning.

### SDM Performance & Monitoring
The team successfully validated the performance of State Diff Management (SDM) on `sdm-net-0`. Benchmarking confirmed that SDM provides significant gas refunds without introducing measurable overhead for builders. To support long-term maintenance, the SDM dashboards were successfully migrated and verified within the OPE Grafana instance ([#22345](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/ecosystem/issues/22345)).

### Interop Repository Consolidation
A significant effort was initiated to reorganize the public developer-facing repositories for OP Interop ([#1008](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/ecosystem/issues/1008)). This refactoring effort aims to:
*   **Refine Scope:** Restate the `ecosystem` repository's focus specifically around `packages/viem` and `packages/wagmi` ([#1011](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/ecosystem/issues/1011)).
*   **Dependency Management:** Resolve staleness in the `interop-lib` Solidity dependencies ([#1009](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/ecosystem/issues/1009)) and upgrade `supersim` to align with the latest OP Stack releases ([#1013](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/ecosystem/issues/1013)).
*   **Code Deletion:** Remove superseded relayer code ([#1010](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/ecosystem/issues/1010)) and evaluate the deprecation of `super-cli` ([#1012](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/ecosystem/issues/1012)).

## 🐛 Issues & Triage
The project saw a high volume of triage activity aimed at resolving legacy issues and identifying critical path blockers for Interop.

### Closed Issues
*   **Node Testing:** The `TestSyncAfterInitialELSync` for `kona-node` was closed following the decision that this specific synchronization path will not be supported in Kona ([#22278](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/ecosystem/issues/22278)).
*   **Benchmarking:** Baseline benchmarks for SDM were finalized, confirming previous efficiency results ([#22344](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/ecosystem/issues/22344)).

### New & Active Issues
*   **RPC Blocking Defect:** A critical issue persists in `op-interop-filter` ([#21943](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/ecosystem/issues/21943)), where the verdict RPC blocks behind an ingester write lock. This leads to fail-closed timeouts rather than immediate rejections for invalid transactions, representing a potential bottleneck for Interop reliability.
*   **Ecosystem Cleanup:** A suite of new issues was opened to audit documentation ([#1014](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/ecosystem/issues/1014)), consolidate starter examples ([#1015](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/ecosystem/issues/1015)), and perform a broad archive sweep of abandoned repositories dating back to 2024 ([#1017](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/ecosystem/issues/1017)).

## 💬 Community & Collaboration
Collaboration this month was heavily focused on cross-repository maintenance and documentation standards. The opening of the Interop Public Repo Cleanup epic indicates a coordinated effort to improve the onboarding experience for external contributors by removing "dead" code and stale examples. Technical discussions regarding the `op-interop-filter` suggest active debugging and testing cycles, with automated agents or contributors like `claude` providing detailed logs of defect occurrences to assist maintainers in identifying race conditions within the ingester logic.
