# ethereum-optimism/infra Monthly Report (February 2026)

## 🚀 Highlights
In February 2026, development focused heavily on hardening the reliability of `proxyd` and refining the infrastructure supporting the Superchain ecosystem. Key achievements included the integration of advanced health-checking and drain functionalities into the proxy service, alongside critical fixes for Ethereum spec compliance regarding block requests. Significant architectural discussions also took place regarding `op-supernode` state management and the integration of Flashblocks into `op-geth`, ensuring the infrastructure remains robust as interoperability features evolve.

## 🛠️ Key Developments

### `proxyd` Reliability and Compliance
The proxy service received substantial updates to improve its resilience and adherence to Ethereum JSON-RPC standards.
*   **Healthcheck and Drain Functionality:** A major enhancement was introduced to `proxyd`, adding configurable HTTP probe workers. This allows the service to perform backend health checking and automatically exclude failing backends, significantly improving system uptime ([#534](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/infra/pull/534)).
*   **Block Request Handling:** Developers addressed issues with out-of-range block requests. A new fix ensures that `proxyd` forwards these requests to the backend rather than failing prematurely ([#547](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/infra/pull/547)).

### Infrastructure Maintenance and Tooling
*   **Branch Management:** Ongoing synchronization of the codebase was maintained through rebasing the `release` branch onto `main` ([#545](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/infra/pull/545)).
*   **Flashblocks Integration:** Progress was made toward supporting Flashblocks within `op-geth`, with community-provided versions now including Firehose Live Tracer and Flashblock support ([#756](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/infra/issues/756)).

## 🐛 Issues & Triage

### New & Active Issues
*   **`proxyd` Spec Compliance:** A new issue was identified where `proxyd` incorrectly returns HTTP/JSON errors for unfound blocks. To align with Ethereum specifications, the service should return a `null` response ([#546](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/infra/issues/546)). This is currently causing friction in CI workflows for `superchain-ops` during fork testing ([#17688](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/infra/issues/17688)).
*   **`op-reth` Snapshot Inconsistencies:** Discussions are ongoing regarding the naming conventions and symlink updates for `op-reth` snapshots. Current inconsistencies in file naming (archival vs. full) and potential failures in symlink updates for "latest" snapshots are being tracked ([#536](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/infra/issues/536)).
*   **Withdrawal Validation Gaps:** An analysis of `BlockNotFoundError` during L1 withdrawal proving suggested a discrepancy between the Optimism SDK and Viem. While the SDK validates output roots, Viem may not, leading to errors when state roots are not yet published ([#1956](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/infra/issues/1956)).

## 💬 Community & Collaboration
The month was characterized by deep technical coordination on protocol-level architecture. A significant discussion emerged around `op-supernode` and its "safe label" adjusting mechanism. Contributors like `geoknee` are advocating for the Consensus Layer (CL) to remain the sole controller of the engine to prevent stateful controllers from falling out of sync, particularly during interop resets ([#18976](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/infra/issues/18976)). Additionally, collaboration with external tool maintainers (such as the `op-geth` variants provided by `maoueh`) continues to drive the adoption of cutting-edge features like Flashblocks.
