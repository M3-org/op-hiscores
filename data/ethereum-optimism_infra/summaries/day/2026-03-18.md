### op-node: Initial EL Sync UX and Correctness [#17631](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/infra/issues/17631)
The discussion on the initial EL Sync UX and correctness for `op-node` continued, focusing on the incorrect behavior where the node marks the reached target as finalized even if it's not actually finalized by the L1 finalization gadget.

**Key Points:**
- **Correctness vs. UX:** The current implementation is incorrect because it sets safe and finalized heads equal to the unsafe head after initial EL sync, instead of respecting L1 finalization.
- **Proposed Solutions:**
    1.  **Set safe/finalized to genesis:** Simplest and most correct, but leads to long derivation times.
    2.  **Set safe/finalized to (unsafe head minus sequencer window):** Offers a balance between correctness and UX, requiring derivation of approximately 12 hours of data. This is the preferred option.
    3.  **External source for safe/finalized:** Best UX but adds complexity and relies on trusted external values.
- **Current Status:** Option 2 is seen as a reasonable compromise. The node will still need time to derive forward to catch up with the L1-dictated safe and finalized tip.

### Non-terminating CI job: memory-all-opn-op-geth [#19563](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/infra/issues/19563)
The `memory-all-opn-op-geth` CI job continues to experience non-terminating issues, with `TestChallengerPlaysGame` and `TestProposer` subtests timing out.

**Key Points:**
- **Reopening:** The issue was reopened after the job hung again, with `TestChallengerPlaysGame` and `TestProposer` hitting the 2-hour subtest timeout.
- **Root Cause (Initial Investigation):** The `waitForClaim` and `WaitForCountered` polling loops in `interop/proofs` create a 20-minute timeout context, but internal RPC calls use the base test context with a 2-hour deadline. This can lead to indefinite blocking if RPC calls to the in-memory L1 geth hang due to CI resource contention.
- **Proposed Fix:** Create a context-bound copy of the `FaultDisputeGame` binding for polling loops to ensure RPC calls respect the per-wait timeout. A draft fix is in progress.
- **Additional Flakes:** Other tests like `TestChallengerRespondsToMultipleInvalidClaims` and `TestChallengerRespondsToMultipleInvalidClaimsEOA` also show flakiness, indicating a broader issue with acceptance tests creating full L1+L2+supernode systems per test, leading to cumulative runtime exceeding job timeouts.

### Flaky test: TestSupernodeSameTimestampInvalidTransitive [#19570](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/infra/issues/19570)
The `TestSupernodeSameTimestampInvalidTransitive` test is consistently timing out, indicating a deeper issue with the supernode's invalidation and rewind mechanism.

**Key Points:**
- **Failure Mode:** Consistent timeout at approximately 2m46s with the message "super-root at timestamp X was not validated in time".
- **Root Cause:** A race condition in multi-chain transitive invalidation. When the supernode detects an invalid message and rewinds, it attempts to insert a synthetic payload. If a previously rejected block has poisoned geth's block rejection cache, the synthetic payload is rejected with "links to previously rejected block". The `RewindEngine` then retries indefinitely because this specific error is not considered critical.
- **Why Flaky:** The race condition depends on the timing and order of invalidations across multiple chains, leading to inconsistent failures.
- **Potential Fixes:**
    1.  Treat `ErrRewindSyntheticPayloadRejected` (or its sub-case) as a critical error to prevent infinite retries.
    2.  Clear geth's block rejection cache before rewinding to remove stale state.
    3.  Implement a different rewind strategy if synthetic payload insertion fails.
    4.  Coordinate multi-chain rewinds to avoid cross-chain cache poisoning.
