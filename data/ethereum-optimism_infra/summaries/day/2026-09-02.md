## ACTIVE ISSUES
### Addressing `op-core/superchain` Module Consumability
Discussion continued on [#22678](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/infra/issues/22678), concerning the `op-core/superchain` module being unconsumable by downstream Go modules due to `superchain-configs.zip` being gitignored.

- **Latest Status and Impact:** The issue remains unresolved, with `superchain-configs.zip` still gitignored and absent from the published Go module. Recent PRs ([#22700](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/optimism/pull/22700) and [#22709](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/optimism/pull/22709)) partially addressed the problem by reducing the number of affected packages, but core registry consumers like `op-deployer`, `op-conductor`, and `op-challenger` are still unbuildable by downstream Go modules.
- **Correction on Supersim:** It was clarified that `supersim` is no longer blocked by this issue, as its dependency path to the bundle was removed in [#22709](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/optimism/pull/22709).
- **Proposed Solutions and Costs:** Previous discussions highlighted that committing the zip directly would incur a cost of approximately 3 MB/year in `.git` growth, which is considered manageable given the impact of the current problem. `git-lfs` is not a viable option due to `cmd/go` and `proxy.golang.org` limitations.
- **Reducing Bundle Reach:** Efforts are underway to reduce the number of packages that accidentally depend on the bundle, with PRs like [#22709](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/optimism/pull/22709) and [#22712](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/optimism/pull/22712) aiming to clean up dependencies and prevent new accidental edges.
