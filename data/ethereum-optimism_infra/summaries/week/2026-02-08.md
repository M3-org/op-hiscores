# ethereum-optimism/infra Weekly Report (Feb 8 - 14, 2026)

## 🚀 Highlights
This week, development focused heavily on the consolidation of Rust-based components within the monorepo, significantly streamlining the `op-reth` development environment. Key infrastructure improvements included enhancing test reliability for `op-acceptor` and resolving cross-platform build issues for Windows users. While the team successfully activated blob priority fee tracking, ongoing technical discussions highlight a focus on optimizing Kona hint formats and refining risk modeling for L2CM upgrades.

## 🛠️ Key Developments

### Rust Monorepo Consolidation & Documentation
A major effort was completed to unify the Rust ecosystem within the Optimism infrastructure. This included:
*   **Workspace Unification:** All Rust crates were consolidated into a single workspace, and `zepter` configurations were unified across repositories to ensure dependency consistency ([#18930](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/infra/issues/18930), [#18931](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/infra/issues/18931)).
*   **op-reth Integration:** The `reth` submodule was removed in favor of a direct `op-reth` integration, with CI updated accordingly ([#18919](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/infra/issues/18919)).
*   **Documentation:** OP Stack-specific documentation was successfully ported from upstream repositories to the local `op-reth` directory ([#18969](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/infra/issues/18969)).

### Build System & Cross-Platform Support
The team addressed several bottlenecks in the build and deployment pipeline:
*   **Windows Compatibility:** Fixed a critical bug in `proxyd` by implementing OS-dependent `syscall` calls, ensuring the tool builds correctly on Windows ([#549](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/infra/pull/549)).
*   **Tooling Updates:** Integrated `goreleaser` with `mise` to streamline installations and ensure environment consistency for contributors ([#550](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/infra/pull/550)).

### Testing Infrastructure
*   **op-acceptor Log Persistence:** Improved the `op-acceptor` test runner to prevent log loss during test timeouts or unexpected process terminations. By directing stdout to temporary files, developers can now reliably debug failures even when processes are killed ([#548](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/infra/pull/548)).

## 🐛 Issues & Triage

### Closed Issues
*   **Blob Fee Tracking:** The activation of `op-batcher/txmgr` blob priority fee tracking was finalized following a fix in the core optimism repository ([#18737](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/infra/issues/18737)).
*   **Infrastructure Setup:** Snapshot configurations for `op-reth`, including naming conventions and symlinks for `datadirs.optimism.io`, were completed ([#536](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/infra/issues/536)).

### New & Active Issues
*   **Kona Optimization Debate:** A significant discussion is underway regarding whether to move Kona hints from string serialization to a binary format ([#19125](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/infra/issues/19125)). While proponents argue for architectural "correctness," skeptics have raised concerns regarding backward compatibility for existing games.
*   **Sequencer Redundancy:** Discussion continues on [#763](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/infra/issues/763) regarding support for multiple sequencer RPC endpoints. The community is weighing the benefits of client-side failover against the simplicity of a single, load-balanced URL.
*   **Technical Debt:** A new issue was opened to resolve a cyclic dependency in `op-deployer` caused by hardcoded hex values for development feature flags ([#19151](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/infra/issues/19151)).
*   **Risk Modeling:** Failure Mode Analysis (FMA) for the L2CM design is ongoing, with templates being established to guide the risk assessment process ([#18869](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/infra/issues/18869)).

## 💬 Community & Collaboration
The week was characterized by deep technical coordination between maintainers on protocol-level changes. Notable collaboration occurred between @ajsutton and @kien-rise regarding the Kona hint format, demonstrating a rigorous peer-review process for architectural changes. Additionally, @maurelian provided critical guidance on risk modeling for L2CM, leveraging existing OPCMv2 documentation to maintain high security standards across the Superchain infrastructure. There is also a clear focus on developer experience, as evidenced by the decision to retain certain `just` recipes for local prestate building despite the migration to the monorepo ([#18654](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/infra/issues/18654)).
