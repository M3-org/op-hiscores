Today's focus was heavily on the L2 Contracts Manager (L2CM) upgrade process, with significant progress on its implementation and extensive discussions around security, testing, and bundle generation across both `op-geth` and `optimism` repositories. Key achievements include the implementation of the L2CM contract itself and a major refactoring of the `op-devstack` Orchestrator, while ongoing discussions address critical aspects like upgrade atomicity, deterministic bundle generation, and comprehensive fork testing.

## ✅ Completed Work
### L2 Contracts Manager (L2CM) Implementation
- The `L2ContractsManager` contract was implemented in `optimism`, enabling upgrades of L2 predeploys via `DELEGATECALL` from ProxyAdmin, a crucial step for flexible L2 contract management. ([#19111](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/optimism/pull/19111))

### Development Stack Refactoring & CI/Supernode Enhancements
- The `op-devstack` Orchestrator was significantly refactored in `optimism`, migrating from 15 separate registry fields to a single unified `*stack.Registry`, streamlining the ID type system. ([#18875](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/optimism/pull/18875))
- Various `contracts-bedrock` CI issues were fixed in `optimism`, improving the stability and reliability of the continuous integration pipeline. ([#19300](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/optimism/pull/19300))
- Duplicated lifecycle management was eliminated and proper application context closure was ensured in `op-supernode` and `op-devstack` within `optimism`, enhancing supernode robustness. ([#19310](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/optimism/pull/19310), [#19306](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/optimism/pull/19306), [#19305](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/optimism/pull/19305))
