# Overall Project Weekly Summary (Jun 7 - 13, 2026)

This week, Optimism reached a major milestone in its architectural evolution by completing the transition to a more modern, Rust-based system for securing the network. By streamlining our codebase and hardening the "dispute games" that protect the integrity of the Superchain, we have made the ecosystem more secure, modular, and ready for future growth.

## Executive Summary
The project successfully finalized the removal of legacy components to favor a more efficient and secure fault-proof system. At the same time, we made significant strides in "decoupling" our core technology from standard Ethereum software, allowing us to customize fee logic and cross-chain messaging specifically for the needs of the Superchain.

### Key Strategic Initiatives & Outcomes

**Modernizing Security and Fault Proofs**
*Goal: To move away from older, complex systems toward a more streamlined and secure way of proving that transactions are valid.*
*   The project completed the removal of the legacy `op-program` from the main codebase in [ethereum-optimism/optimism](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/optimism), officially shifting focus to the more modern `rust/kona` implementation.
*   Security was hardened in [ethereum-optimism/superchain-ops](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/superchain-ops) by fixing a bug in how "dispute games" are deactivated, ensuring the system resets cleanly and prevents future errors.

**Building a Modular and Independent Protocol**
*Goal: To separate Optimism’s unique features from standard Ethereum code, making the system easier to maintain and upgrade.*
*   We launched a new "core" package to centralize how the network calculates fees and manages chain settings, reducing our dependence on external software in [ethereum-optimism/optimism](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/optimism).
*   Technical specifications were updated in [ethereum-optimism/specs](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/specs) to ensure that fee math is perfectly accurate, preventing the network from undercounting the costs of storing data.

**Enhancing Cross-Chain Reliability**
*Goal: To ensure that different chains in the Superchain can talk to each other safely and reliably.*
*   The "proxyd" service was updated in [ethereum-optimism/infra](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/infra) to include a "fail-closed" security default, meaning the system will stop a transaction if it cannot be 100% verified, rather than risking an error.
*   New tools were introduced in [ethereum-optimism/optimism](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/optimism) to allow developers to "smoke-test" messages as they travel between different chains, ensuring features like ETH bridging work as intended.

### Cross-Repository Coordination
**The "Geth Decoupling" Effort**
This week required intense coordination across **specs**, **infra**, and **optimism** to separate our unique fee-calculation logic from the standard Ethereum execution engine (Geth).
*   **Goal**: To create a standalone math library (`op-core/fees`) that works the same way across all our tools.
*   **Result**: By coordinating changes in the technical [specs](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/specs) with implementation in the [monorepo](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/optimism) and [infrastructure](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/infra) tools, we ensured that transaction cost estimates remain accurate even as we move to a more modular architecture.
