# Overall Project Weekly Summary (May 24 - 30, 2026)

This week, the Optimism ecosystem focused on "hardening" the foundation of the Superchain by aggressively tackling technical debt and improving infrastructure security. While the team advanced next-generation designs for Zero-Knowledge proofs and predictive deployments, the primary success was a rigorous cleanup of the codebase to ensure long-term stability and reliability for developers and users alike.

## Executive Summary
The overarching goal this week was to transition from rapid feature development to a phase of high-intensity maintenance and security hardening. The team successfully streamlined the project's digital footprint through container optimizations and the removal of legacy components, while simultaneously initiating a deep-dive investigation into cross-client synchronization to ensure the Superchain remains resilient as it scales.

### Key Strategic Initiatives & Outcomes

**Hardening Infrastructure and Security**
*Goal: To make the platform more secure against attacks and more efficient to run.*
*   The project migrated core service images to "Chainguard" in [ethereum-optimism/op-geth](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/op-geth) and [ethereum-optimism/infra](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/infra), resulting in a smaller, more secure, and faster-loading environment for node operators.
*   A new "standby cluster" command was added to [ethereum-optimism/infra](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/infra), allowing operators to safely set up backup environments without risking live network stability.
*   The community was alerted to security risks regarding the use of AI tools for debugging, protecting proprietary rollup configurations from potential data leaks.

**Improving Developer Experience and Tooling**
*Goal: To provide builders with more reliable tools and a clearer path for deploying applications.*
*   New proposals were introduced in [ethereum-optimism/superchain-registry](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/superchain-registry) to support "Deployment Factories," which will help developers better track how their smart contracts are connected and deployed.
*   The team addressed "nondeterministic" build issues in [ethereum-optimism/optimism](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/optimism) and other repos, ensuring that when developers compile their code, the results are consistent every time.
*   Documentation was updated to establish `op-reth` as the primary execution layer, providing a clearer technical roadmap for the community in [ethereum-optimism/optimism](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/optimism).

**Scaling Ecosystem Governance and Trust**
*Goal: To ensure funding and governance are directed toward real users rather than automated bots.*
*   Work began on a "blended user score" in [ethereum-optimism/Retro-Funding](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/Retro-Funding) and the registry, moving beyond simple social IDs to include on-chain activity and identity attestations to improve Sybil resistance.
*   The Superchain Registry was updated in [ethereum-optimism/superchain-registry](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/superchain-registry) to reflect the current ecosystem state, including the activation of the Karst hardfork and the removal of sunsetting chains.

### Cross-Repository Coordination
This week required intense synchronization between protocol, infrastructure, and execution client teams to solve deep-seated technical hurdles.
*   **Execution Client Stability**: Teams across [ethereum-optimism/op-geth](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/op-geth), [infra](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/infra), and [optimism](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/optimism) coordinated with the Reth core team to diagnose "Engine API" stalls and system crashes. This joint effort is critical for ensuring that different software clients can talk to each other without the network "stalling."
*   **Codebase Hygiene**: A massive, coordinated effort across nearly all repositories (including [Retro-Funding](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/Retro-Funding) and [design-docs](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/design-docs)) saw the reopening of dozens of issues to address "TODO" comments. This ensures that temporary workarounds used during early development are replaced with permanent, production-ready solutions.
*   **Component Retirement**: The `op-supervisor` component was successfully removed across the entire monorepo and supporting repositories, simplifying the architecture for all contributors.
