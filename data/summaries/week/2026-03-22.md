# Overall Project Weekly Summary (Mar 22 - 28, 2026)

This week, Optimism made significant strides in securing the Superchain by finalizing the safety blueprints for Zero-Knowledge (ZK) technology and upgrading core infrastructure to the latest industry standards. We also successfully cleared a major backlog of testing hurdles, ensuring that the platform remains stable and reliable as we expand our cross-chain capabilities.

## Executive Summary
The primary focus this week was "hardening" the ecosystem—making it more secure against attacks and more reliable for developers. We achieved this by integrating new ZK fault-proof specifications, migrating to more secure "hardened" server images, and resolving a series of persistent technical glitches in our automated testing suites.

### Key Strategic Initiatives & Outcomes

**Advancing Toward a More Secure, Multi-Proof Future**
*Goal: To increase the security of the Superchain by using multiple types of mathematical proofs to verify transactions.*
*   We introduced formal technical requirements and smart contract mechanics for ZK dispute games in [ethereum-optimism/specs](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/specs/pull/896).
*   A comprehensive "Failure Mode Analysis" was completed in [ethereum-optimism/design-docs](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/design-docs/pull/369), identifying 30 specific actions to protect the system from potential vulnerabilities.
*   The legacy "Optimistic" ZK game was replaced with a new, spec-compliant version in [ethereum-optimism/optimism](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/optimism/pull/19606), bringing us closer to a production-ready ZK proof system.

**Strengthening Infrastructure and Developer Reliability**
*Goal: To ensure the tools and servers powering Optimism are modern, secure, and free of "flaky" errors that slow down development.*
*   The entire project was upgraded to Go 1.26 in [ethereum-optimism/infra](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/infra/pull/579), ensuring we are using the fastest and most secure version of our primary programming language.
*   We migrated to "Hardened" Docker images in [ethereum-optimism/infra](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/infra/pull/580), removing unnecessary software components to reduce the "attack surface" for hackers.
*   A major push to fix "flaky" tests resulted in the closure of several long-standing issues across [ethereum-optimism/optimism](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/optimism/issues/19672) and [ethereum-optimism/docs](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/docs/issues/19586), meaning developers can now build and ship code with more confidence.

**Expanding Cross-Chain Interoperability**
*Goal: To make it easier for different blockchains in the Superchain to talk to each other and share assets seamlessly.*
*   We integrated essential bridge contracts for moving ETH between chains into the automated network upgrade path in [ethereum-optimism/optimism](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/optimism/pull/19648).
*   New testing tools were created in [ethereum-optimism/superchain-ops](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/superchain-ops/pull/19726) that use real-world data to verify cross-chain communication without the long wait times of traditional tests.

### Cross-Repository Coordination
This week required intense synchronization between our protocol, infrastructure, and security teams to resolve a critical "panic" (a type of software crash) affecting the **Kona guest program**.
*   **The Problem**: A missing piece of data in the "chain list" was causing the system to crash during proof generation.
*   **The Coordination**: This was identified and resolved through coordinated updates across [ethereum-optimism/ecosystem](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/ecosystem/issues/19654), [ethereum-optimism/infra](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/infra/issues/19654), and [ethereum-optimism/optimism](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/optimism/issues/19654).
*   **The Result**: The fix ensures that the Superchain registry can be read reliably, preventing crashes for users and developers interacting with the Cannon VM.
