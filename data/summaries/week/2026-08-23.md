# Overall Project Weekly Summary (Aug 23 - 29, 2026)

This week, Optimism focused on streamlining the Superchain ecosystem by cleaning up legacy code and hardening the core technology that powers our network. We made significant strides in decentralizing chain ownership and improving the reliability of our cross-chain communication tools, ensuring a more stable and professional experience for both developers and users.

## Executive Summary
The primary focus this week was a major "spring cleaning" of the Superchain ecosystem, consolidating developer resources and archiving abandoned projects to reduce confusion. Simultaneously, we successfully transitioned the ownership of several major chains to independent operators, marking a significant milestone in our journey toward a decentralized Superchain.

### Key Strategic Initiatives & Outcomes

**Simplifying the Developer Experience**
*Goal: We want to make it as easy as possible for new builders to join the Optimism ecosystem by providing clear, up-to-date resources.*
*   Initiated a major consolidation of "starter" repositories into a single, high-quality entry point to reduce fragmentation across the ecosystem ([ethereum-optimism/infra#1015](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/infra/issues/1015)).
*   Completed a comprehensive audit to archive obsolete tools and repositories, ensuring that public documentation only points to supported and secure code ([ethereum-optimism/ecosystem#1014](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/ecosystem/issues/1014)).
*   Updated the Superchain Registry to reflect the addition of "Main's World" to the World Chain, keeping our ecosystem map accurate as new partners join ([ethereum-optimism.github.io#1356](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/ethereum-optimism.github.io/pull/1356)).

**Advancing Decentralization and Governance**
*Goal: To build a resilient network, we are transferring control of key infrastructure to community partners and improving our deployment tools.*
*   Successfully handed over ownership of the Metal, Mode, Zora, and Dust chains to Conduit, demonstrating our ability to transition chain management to independent operators ([ethereum-optimism/superchain-ops#1521](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/superchain-ops/pull/1521)).
*   Enhanced our ownership transfer tools to allow multiple security updates to happen in a single step, making governance more efficient ([ethereum-optimism/superchain-ops#1519](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/superchain-ops/pull/1519)).
*   Validated the full lifecycle of a new chain deployment on the Plataberget testnet, proving that our automated deployment tools are ready for the next generation of L2s ([ethereum-optimism/superchain-ops#22696](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/superchain-ops/issues/22696)).

**Hardening Protocol Security and Reliability**
*Goal: We are identifying and fixing complex technical bugs to ensure the network remains stable even under heavy load or unexpected errors.*
*   Modernized the networking stack in `op-geth` to improve how nodes find and talk to each other, reducing the risk of connection drops ([ethereum-optimism/op-geth#807](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/op-geth/pull/807)).
*   Introduced new monitoring tools to track how long it takes for transactions to be included in the blockchain, giving us better data on the actual user experience ([ethereum-optimism/infra#726](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/infra/pull/726)).
*   Identified and began fixing a critical "looping" issue in the Kona sequencer that could cause high CPU usage during network reorganizations ([ethereum-optimism/optimism#22681](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/optimism/issues/22681)).

### Cross-Repository Coordination
*   **Superchain Starter Consolidation**: This effort spanned `infra`, `ecosystem`, `specs`, and `superchain-ops`. By auditing links across all these areas, the team ensured that merging five different "starter" repos into one would not break existing tutorials or developer workflows.
*   **Kona Protocol Hardening**: Coordination between the `optimism` monorepo and `specs` was vital this week to address sequencer stability. While engineers worked on code fixes for P2P stalling ([ethereum-optimism/specs#22646](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/specs/issues/22646)), they simultaneously updated the protocol rules to ensure these fixes align with our long-term interoperability goals.
