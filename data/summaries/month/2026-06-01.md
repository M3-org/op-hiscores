# Overall Project Monthly Summary (June 2026)

## Executive Summary
This month, Optimism reached a major turning point by officially transitioning to the **Lagoon** (formerly Interop) protocol and moving toward a more powerful execution engine. While successfully proving that cross-chain withdrawals work on our new infrastructure, the team also identified and began fixing deep-seated technical "deadlocks" to ensure the Superchain remains stable and fast as it grows.

### Key Strategic Initiatives & Outcomes

**Advancing the Superchain Interoperability Layer**
*Goal: To create a seamless experience where multiple blockchains work together as one unified network.*
*   Successfully proved that users can securely withdraw funds across different chains in a test environment, a foundational step for the Superchain's security ([ethereum-optimism/superchain-registry#20489](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/superchain-registry/issues/20489)).
*   Renamed the "Interop" hardfork to **Lagoon**, aligning all technical parts of the ecosystem under a single, stable release candidate ([ethereum-optimism/optimism#21105](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/optimism/pull/21105)).
*   Introduced "Safer Safes" to protect community funds with better recovery options and security locks ([ethereum-optimism/specs#914](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/specs/pull/914)).

**Hardening Infrastructure for Reliability**
*Goal: To ensure the network stays online and recovers quickly from unexpected errors.*
*   Identified and developed fixes for a complex "deadlock" issue that was causing nodes to freeze during synchronization ([ethereum-optimism/design-docs#21076](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/design-docs/issues/21076)).
*   Shifted from automated "experimental" fixes to human-led "runbooks" for complex network repairs, prioritizing safety and accuracy over speed during critical events ([ethereum-optimism/infra#20006](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/infra/issues/20006)).
*   Implemented protocol-level limits on sequencer refunds to prevent potential "denial of service" attacks that could slow down the network ([ethereum-optimism/specs#20709](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/specs/issues/20709)).

**Modernizing the Developer and Operator Experience**
*Goal: To make it easier and faster for developers to build on and run Optimism nodes.*
*   Deprecated the older `op-geth` system in favor of the more modern `op-reth` for the latest protocol updates, streamlining the technology stack ([ethereum-optimism/optimism#21188](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/optimism/pull/21188)).
*   Significantly reduced the time it takes to run automated tests by optimizing how the system starts up, helping developers catch bugs faster ([ethereum-optimism/specs#21191](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/specs/issues/21191)).
*   Improved build reliability by fixing issues in our "container" system, ensuring that the software installs correctly every time ([ethereum-optimism/ethereum-optimism.github.io#1324](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/ethereum-optimism.github.io/pull/1324)).

### Cross-Repository Coordination
*   **Lagoon Hardfork Alignment**: The transition to the Lagoon protocol required a massive, synchronized update across the registry, core protocol code, and documentation to ensure all chains in the Superchain stay in sync ([ethereum-optimism/optimism#21105](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/optimism/pull/21105)).
*   **Execution Engine Migration**: The shift to `op-reth` involved coordinated work between the infrastructure, specs, and design teams to ensure the new engine could handle the complex requirements of the Superchain ([ethereum-optimism/design-docs#21069](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/design-docs/issues/21069)).
*   **Upstream Collaboration**: Optimism engineers worked closely with the Paradigm team to fix low-level bugs in the `reth` engine, benefiting the broader Ethereum ecosystem ([ethereum-optimism/superchain-registry#21076](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/superchain-registry/issues/21076)).
