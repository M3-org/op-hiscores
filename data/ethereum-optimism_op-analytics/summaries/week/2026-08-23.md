# ethereum-optimism/op-analytics Weekly Report (Aug 23 - 29, 2026)

## 🚀 Highlights
This week, the `op-analytics` team focused on infrastructure stability and the strategic consolidation of developer resources. Efforts were centered on resolving critical sequencer wedging issues in `kona-node` and investigating interoperability stability within `op-supernode`. A significant shift in data sourcing is underway with a move toward public RPCs for blob base fee tracking, while a comprehensive audit of the Superchain starter ecosystem has paved the way for archiving legacy repositories and streamlining the developer experience.

## 🛠️ Key Developments

### Infrastructure & Data Sourcing
Work began on migrating core data dependencies to more resilient and open sources.
*   **Public RPC Migration:** A new pull request ([#1800](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/op-analytics/pull/1800)) was introduced to replace Blocknative with a public RPC for blob base fee monitoring and to facilitate Eth migration. This move aims to reduce reliance on third-party providers for critical network analytics.

## 🐛 Issues & Triage

### Closed Issues
*   **Supernode Stability:** Investigation into a cross-safe wedge issue ([#22325](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/op-analytics/issues/22325)) was concluded. The team determined the root cause was a rare parent-hash mismatch during invalidation recovery, occurring only once in nearly two months of operation.
*   **Kona Configuration:** The team decided not to change `kona-genesis` behavior regarding unknown `rollup.json` keys ([#22682](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/op-analytics/issues/22682)). The current leniency is intentional to support retired keys, with existing registry tests deemed sufficient for configuration safety.

### New & Active Issues
*   **Sequencer Reliability:** A critical new issue was identified where the `kona-node` sequencer can become permanently wedged during engine reorgs ([#22681](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/op-analytics/issues/22681)). Additionally, work is progressing on addressing `NetworkActor` stalling ([#22646](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/op-analytics/issues/22646)), with contributors planning to test overflow policies and latency injection to confirm fixes.
*   **SuperchainERC20 Compatibility:** A gap was identified in developer tooling for checking `SuperchainERC20` compatibility ([#1020](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/op-analytics/issues/1020)). This has become a high priority due to the potential revival of the `SuperchainTokenBridge` and the requirements for the Ink network.
*   **Repository Lifecycle Management:** A major audit of starter examples and abandoned repositories is underway ([#1014](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/op-analytics/issues/1014), [#1015](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/op-analytics/issues/1015), [#1017](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/op-analytics/issues/1017)). The plan involves consolidating five `superchain-starter` variants into a single repository while archiving obsolete tools to clean up the organization's footprint.

## 💬 Community & Collaboration
The week saw significant analytical contributions from `jefr90`, who provided the technical mapping and migration strategy for the repository consolidation project. There is active coordination between contributors like `jdodinh` and the core maintainers regarding `kona-node` performance, specifically around aligning overflow policies with existing protocol standards. The discussions reflect a cautious, data-driven approach to archiving legacy code, ensuring that no live documentation or developer workflows are disrupted during the transition.
