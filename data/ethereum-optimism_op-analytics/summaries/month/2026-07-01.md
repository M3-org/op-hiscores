# ethereum-optimism/op-analytics Monthly Report (July 2026)

## 🚀 Highlights
In July 2026, development focused on stabilizing the network following the Karst hard fork and refining the project's testing infrastructure. A critical intrinsic gas estimation error on Optimism Sepolia was successfully resolved and deployed via `op-reth`, ensuring reliable transaction simulation for users. Simultaneously, the team conducted a strategic audit of the repository's fuzzing operations, leading to a decision to retire low-value CI fuzzing jobs in favor of more deterministic testing. Efforts also centered on addressing non-deterministic flakes in integration tests and optimizing system configuration interactions to improve overall performance.

## 🛠️ Key Developments

### System Configuration & Performance Optimization
Work this month targeted the efficiency of system configuration interactions, particularly in preparation for version 4.0.0.
*   **Getter Optimization:** A new initiative was launched to stop redundant calls to `SystemConfig` batch inbox getters, aiming to reduce overhead and streamline operations ([#1798](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/op-analytics/pull/1798)).
*   **Compatibility Updates:** The `chainsmeta` component was updated to maintain stability by tolerating the removal of specific getters in `SystemConfig 4.0.0` ([#1799](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/op-analytics/pull/1799)).

### Testing Infrastructure & Fuzzing Strategy
The team performed a deep dive into the effectiveness of current CI testing suites to reduce maintenance overhead and improve reliability.
*   **Fuzzing Audit:** An audit of Go fuzz tests revealed that time-bounded exploration in CI provided minimal value without a committed corpus. The team moved to retire six PR-time fuzzing jobs while retaining the underlying functions as deterministic seed tests ([#21636](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/op-analytics/issues/21636)).
*   **Dependency Management:** Maintenance work included tracking the `revm` dependency for an upcoming update to version 2.4.0 / 2.3.1 to support `op-reth` ([#21524](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/op-analytics/issues/21524)).

## 🐛 Issues & Triage

### Closed Issues
*   **Sepolia Gas Estimation Fix:** The most significant resolution this month addressed a bug where `eth_estimateGas` returned "intrinsic gas too high" errors on OP Sepolia post-Karst hard fork. The fix was integrated into the `op-reth/v2.3.3` release, restoring functionality for RPC providers ([#21457](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/op-analytics/issues/21457)).

### New & Active Issues
*   **Interoperability Test Flakiness:** A new issue was identified in `TestSupernodeLightSequencerInteropInvalidMessageReplacement`. The root cause involves `FollowSource` processing local-safe heads ahead of unsafe heads, leading to stale build states. A draft PR is currently being evaluated to force sequencer resets on upstream divergence ([#21635](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/op-analytics/issues/21635)).
*   **Reth Integration SIGSEGV:** The team continues to investigate a persistent, non-deterministic SIGSEGV (signal 11) occurring during the teardown of `reth-optimism-node` p2p integration tests. This is suspected to be a race condition involving orphan OS threads accessing Tokio internals during runtime shutdown ([#20973](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/op-analytics/issues/20973)).

## 💬 Community & Collaboration
Collaboration this month was characterized by rigorous technical auditing and cross-component troubleshooting. The resolution of the Sepolia gas issue demonstrated effective coordination between protocol developers and RPC maintainers. Furthermore, the discussion surrounding the retirement of fuzzing jobs ([#21636](https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/op-analytics/issues/21636)) highlighted a community-wide focus on CI efficiency and reducing "test noise," ensuring that contributors can focus on high-impact changes without being hindered by low-value or flaky test failures.
